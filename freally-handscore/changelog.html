v0.6.02026-10-16

Lessons. Handscore now teaches the piano, Day by Day, from your very first notes to about Grade 2: 400 Days in two sections, Beginner and Elementary, each a guided session of its own. The hands show you each exercise from any view, then the course listens as you play on your MIDI keyboard and moves on only when it is right. Switch on Read Aloud and your computer's own voice reads the lesson to you, each word lit as it is spoken.

Added

  • Lessons in the studio. Select Mode → Lessons opens the piano course: the course map and the Day's steps on the left, the stage in the middle, the lesson's text under it. Exit goes back to the song you left.
  • 400 Days of piano. Beginner (Days 1–90) and Elementary (Days 91–400): sitting and hand shape, the five-finger positions, reading the grand staff, rhythm, chords, the pedal, scales and arpeggios, hands together and pieces to play, with a review Day every week and earlier work brought back when it is due.
  • Listen and check, three ways. Try: the keys to play light up and the music waits for them. In time: in tempo after a count-in, every note checked against the beat. Perform: play it through, with a summary at the end.
  • Feedback like a teacher's. Each take names the bar and the beat and gives the fix — a note missed, early or late, joined or detached, the pedal — and says what went well.
  • Practice tools. Loop a few bars, play one hand while the stage plays the other, climb the tempo ladder (a step up after three clean takes, a step back after three misses) and watch slow demonstrations in any view with the finger numbers shown. The key names fade as you learn them.
  • Read Aloud. Off until you switch it on, and remembered. Your computer's voice reads the lesson, the word it is saying lit like a karaoke screen and the text scrolling to keep it in view. Play and pause with Space, stop, hold ⏪ or ⏩ to run back or ahead, set the pace from half speed to double and choose the voice; a clock shows the reading time gone and left.
  • Your progress, kept. The Days you have passed, the time spent in each lesson and in all of them, and the days you practised, in days, hours, minutes and seconds — on this computer, in the standalone and every DAW. Continue opens exactly where you stopped: the Day, the step and the word.

v0.5.02026-10-15

The overhead and side views. Watch the hands from the front, from straight above or from the side, and glide from one view to another while the music plays — the keys, the hands and the lit notes never miss a beat. From above, a grand closes its lid and the sheets lie flat on it, ready to read; from the side, the shape of the hands shows: how each finger curves and how far each key goes down.

Added

  • Three views: Front, Top and Side. Turn the view knob in the stage's top-right corner, or press V for the next view and Shift+V for the one before. Your project remembers the view.
  • A seamless switch. The camera glides from one view to the next without ever passing through the piano, and the music plays on through the move: every key, hand and lit note is exactly where it would be with the camera standing still.
  • Top: the keys and the hands from above. A grand folds its prop, lays its music desk flat and closes its lid; the sheets lift off the desk, wait over the keys and lie down on the closed lid side by side, reading upright from where you sit. An upright's sheets lie on its top lid.
  • Side: the hands in profile. The camera looks along the keyboard from the treble end, at the height of the keys — or from the bass end, with From the left under the view knob — and the sheets turn on the desk toward it so they stay readable.
  • The hands from every side. They cast their shadows on the keys they play, and from above no fingertip ever sinks into a key or the case.
  • A sheet close up in any view. Click it, or press 1 to 5; Esc goes back to the view.
  • Smooth in every view. After each change of view or piano, Handscore checks again how smoothly the stage draws, and if it has slowed down, lowers the quality and says so.

v0.4.02026-10-15

Generate, read and drag. Search any of 73 genres, 1,507 artists or 833 producers, press Generate, and four parts — chords, melody, counter-melody and bassline — are written in that style and played by the hands on the piano. Each part is printed as real sheet music on the piano's music desk, beside a full score, every note lighting as it sounds. Drag a part, or the whole song, straight into your DAW, edit any note in its own piano roll, and export the MIDI or a PDF of the sheets with your credits.

Added

  • Generate in any style. Search a genre, an artist or a producer in the panel on the left (its tab on the app's edge shows and hides it) and press Generate, or G. Chips beside it set the key, the scale (41 of them), the mood, the tempo (20 to 999 BPM; drag up or down on the number), the complexity and 4 or 8 bars, and each reads what the song was made in. Follow DAW writes at your project's tempo.
  • Four parts, played by the hands. Chords, melody, counter-melody and bassline, voiced for two hands (Playable) or exactly as written (Raw). Every generation fills its 4 or 8 bars, and switching 4 to 8 repeats the first four.
  • Real sheet music. Every part printed as a page on the music desk, with its title and page number, and a full score of all the parts beside them. Every note head lights as it sounds under a moving playhead, and a long part turns its pages as it plays. Click a sheet, or press 1 to 5, to bring it close; Esc goes back.
  • The sheet tray. Under the stage, each part with Solo, Mute, Lock and a new take. A locked part stays when you generate again.
  • Drag into your DAW. Drag one part, or the whole song with Drag all, from its sheet into your DAW as MIDI.
  • Export. Save the MIDI of a part or the whole song, with every note's velocity, each part's level and pan, the tempo and the time signature.
  • Export to PDF. A PDF of the sheets with a cover page: the title, tempo, key and scale, length, bars, the notes in total, per part and per page, and the instruments. Add credits — writers, vocalists, producers, the samples you used and where they came from, the record label — with names suggested as you type.
  • A tab for each part. Stage, Melody, Chords, Counter and Bassline along the top, each part's tab with its own piano roll and a mute dot, Solo and a × that clears it.
  • Edit the notes. Select, move, lengthen, delete, copy, paste and clone notes in any part's roll; right-click and drag to take notes away and back. Record from a MIDI keyboard into the roll after a count-in of one or two bars. Each part has its own gain and pan.
  • Scales on the rolls. Fold a roll to the song's scale or to the rows that hold notes; the scale's rows are tinted and a note outside it is marked.
  • One-note chords. Switch them on and one key plays a chord of 3, 4 or 5 notes in the song's key, from the mouse, the computer keyboard or a MIDI keyboard.
  • Ghost notes. As you draw, the next note or chord is suggested faintly; Tab keeps it, a click keeps one, and drawing elsewhere clears it.
  • Lyrics. Type words under the melody or the counter-melody, the syllables marked, and they are printed on the sheet and lit as they are sung. A counter beside each line shows whether it fits its bar. As you type, the rest of the word is suggested (Tab takes it) and a misspelling is corrected, unless you tap the checkmark or press Shift+Tab to keep your word.
  • Take, Dream 1–3 and Re-Dream. Three new versions of any part beside the one you have; pick one and keep it. A classic's Re-Dream flips it into a new song of your own.
  • Seeds. Every generation shows its seed and each part's take seed, with Copy. Paste a song's seed to make that song again, or a part's take seed to bring that take back.
  • History. Back and Forward through every generation you have made, each brought back exactly, kept across restarts. A new generation never repeats an old one.
  • Classics stay yours to learn, never to take. Only a classic's chords may leave it. A generated song whose melody, counter-melody or bassline matches a well-known tune keeps that part in, and says why.
  • The Keyboard Shortcuts dialog. Press ? or its key in the top bar to see every shortcut.
  • Your project remembers. Saving your DAW project keeps the song, its edits, its mix, its words, the piano, the view and the credits, and reopening brings them back as they were.

Changed

  • The timeline's preview is larger, and shows the words being sung at that moment.

v0.3.02026-10-14

The realistic hands. A pair of hands now plays the piano on Handscore's stage the way a pianist would — each finger chosen for the passage, the thumb passing under on a scale, a hand lifting up and over the other when the music crosses. Sixty-two public-domain classics are ready to learn, from Mary Had a Little Lamb to Silent Night and The Star-Spangled Banner, and a transport under the stage lets you pause, rewind, fast-forward or drag to any moment, hearing the notes as you go.

Added

  • Realistic hands at the keyboard. Two hands, ending at the wrist, play every note on all four pianos: each finger chosen as a pianist would choose it, the thumb passing under on scales, wide chords reached, the thumb lying on its side and gently bent, and a hand lifting up and over the other to cross. They rest on the keys, ready, when nothing plays.
  • Fingering worked out before a note sounds. Handscore decides which hand plays each note and with which finger before the music plays, so the hands arrive as the keys go down rather than after.
  • Select Mode. The button at the top left (or P) now reads Select Mode and opens your choices by category: Instruments, Classics, Passages, and Lessons (coming in a later version).
  • Sixty-two classics to learn. Nursery songs, Christmas songs and carols, folk songs, spirituals and anthems, classical themes and piano favourites — Chopsticks, Jingle Bells, Silent Night, Ode to Joy, Für Elise, The Entertainer, The Star-Spangled Banner and more — each arranged for two hands, the melody in the right and the chords in the left. Search them by title or composer. Every one is in the public domain.
  • Passages to watch. A scale, a ii–V–I, an arpeggio and a crossing, each a quick look at the hands at work.
  • The transport. Play, pause, stop, rewind and fast-forward under the stage, with a timeline showing the song's time and its bars. Hover over the timeline to see the hands at that moment in a small preview. Drag along it, or hold rewind or fast-forward, and you hear every note you pass, in either direction.
  • A metronome. Switch it on and a click marks each beat, the first of every bar higher, while the dots beside its M show the beat.
  • Space plays and pauses.

Changed

  • The instrument button is now Select Mode, and the piano you choose is marked Current among the Instruments.

v0.2.02026-10-14

The real pianos. Four pianos now stand on Handscore's stage — a concert grand, a parlor grand, a studio upright and an old saloon upright — each lit like a studio and each with its own voice. Click a key or play from your DAW and the piano plays: the key goes down, its name fades, and the sustain pedal holds what you let go. A picker lets you hear a piano before you choose it.

Added

  • Four real pianos. Grand Piano, Acoustic Piano, Upright Piano and Honky-Tonk Piano, each with its own shape, finish and sound, in both themes.
  • Play the keys. Click a key to hear it, or slide across the keys to play each in turn. MIDI from your DAW plays the piano and moves its keys, and the sustain pedal holds the notes you let go until you lift it.
  • Keys that move like a piano's. Each key goes down on its balance rail, faster the harder it is played, and its name fades while it is down and comes back when it rises.
  • The piano picker. The instrument button at the top left (or P) opens four cards. Preview plays a short phrase on that piano without switching; Choose puts it on the stage, crossfading from the last one. Your choice is saved with your project.
  • Quality that fits your computer. Handscore measures how fast your computer draws the stage and settles on the quality it can hold smoothly.

Fixed

  • Keys keep up on a busy computer. A key played while the computer was slow to draw could stay up, or its name stay half-faded; it now always ends where your playing left it.

v0.1.02026-10-12

The foundation. Freally Handscore loads as a CLAP and VST3 plugin and as a standalone app, asks you to accept its licence, and opens on its stage: an 88-key keyboard at a real piano's measurements, with every key named on its front. Nothing is generated yet — that arrives with the real pianos and the hands.

Added

  • The plugin and the standalone app. CLAP and VST3 on Windows (the AU is built on a Mac), and a standalone app that wears Handscore's icon in its title bar and taskbar.
  • The licence agreement comes first. Until you agree, Handscore does nothing. Your answer is kept on your computer and shared by every open copy, in every DAW and in the standalone app.
  • The stage. An 88-key keyboard, A0 to C8, at a real piano's measurements, filling the width of the window. Every key's name is on its front — white keys in dark ink, black keys in white — and hides while the key is down.
  • Light and dark. The switch at the top right; Handscore remembers your choice.
  • Eighteen languages. Handscore starts in your system's language.
  • A 3D view that says when it cannot draw. A window without WebGL 2 explains how to turn it on instead of showing nothing.
  • Crashes are kept, never sent. If Handscore ever stops unexpectedly, a short report is kept on your computer for the next launch. Nothing leaves your computer unless you choose to report it.
