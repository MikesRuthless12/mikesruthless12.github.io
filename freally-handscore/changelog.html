v0.14.02026-10-24

The bass. Put a bass guitar, a slap bass or an upright bass on stage and watch a bassist's hands play your bassline: fingerstyle with two fingers walking, the slap thumb and the pop, the upright plucked from the side of the finger, an 808 line's slides gliding along the string. Play on four strings or five. Two new rock guitars scream through an amp of Handscore's own. The bass course teaches the bass in Days 1 to 180, fingerstyle first, then slap.

Added

  • Three basses. The Pluck Bass, the Slap Bass and the Upright Bass, in Select Mode under Instruments on the new Basses tab. A bass plays the Bass part; Backing chooses whether the other parts still sound.
  • A bassist's hands. The fretting hand plays one finger a fret in the high positions and index, middle and little finger in the low ones, as bassists are taught, moving position on a guide finger; on the upright that fingering holds everywhere, the fingertip on the fingerboard at the note's own place. The plucking hand alternates two fingers, rakes across the strings, slaps with the thumb and pops with a finger, and plays ghost notes. Only the hands are drawn.
  • 808 slides on the bass. A generated 808 line's slides are plucked once and glide along the string fret by fret. Dragged or exported, the bass part writes each slide as the overlapping pair of notes a sampler hears as a glide.
  • Four or five strings. Settings, Instrument, Strings: the Pluck and the Slap Bass play on four strings or on five, with a low B. On four strings a phrase that reaches below the low E moves up an octave as one; on five it plays at its own pitch. Either way the part you drag or export is the bassline as it was generated.
  • Two screaming guitars. The Overdriven Guitar (crunchy classic hard rock) and the Distortion Guitar (high-gain metal), on the Guitars tab, played through Handscore's own amp: a tube-style preamp, a tone stack, a power amp and a cabinet. They play power chords, the Distortion Guitar's palm-muted, on a rock guitar of their own.
  • Bass sheets. A bass's sheet shows the bass clef over four-line TAB (five lines on five strings), with ghost notes, slides, and the slap's T and pop's P under the TAB.
  • The bass course. Days 1 to 180 on the Pluck Bass: reading the bass clef and TAB, the two-finger walk, roots, fifths and octaves, scales, arpeggios and grooves, walking lines, and from Day 151 slap and pop on the Slap Bass, with the dots on the neck and Listen hearing your own bass.
  • Dots on the bass's neck. In a bass lesson each coming note shows as a dot on its string and fret, its finger's number inside, as on the guitars.

Changed

  • A new cursor. A ring with a dot at its heart replaces the arrow and the pointing hand: the dot is where you point, the ring follows it, grows over anything you can press and shrinks while you press. Every other cursor, such as the text cursor and the resize arrows, stays as it was.
  • Toggles light green. Every toggle is gray when off, white when you point at it, and green when on, whether or not you point at it. Mute and Solo on the Arrangement's parts follow it too.

v0.13.02026-10-23

The guitars. Put a steel-string, a classical, an electric or a palm-muted guitar on stage and watch a guitarist's hands play your loop: chords in the shapes a guitarist uses, strummed, picked or fingerpicked by the style, lines fretted where they lie best on the neck, hammer-ons, pull-offs and slides where a real player would use them. The strings are pressed down onto the frets and vibrate as they ring, and the sheets show TAB with chord diagrams. Choose the part the guitar plays and whether the others still sound. Two guitar courses, contemporary and classical, teach the guitar in Days 1 to 150 each, the dots on the neck showing where your fingers go next.

Added

  • Four guitars. The Guitar (steel-string), the Nylon Guitar, the Electric Guitar and the Muted Guitar, in Select Mode under Instruments on the new Guitars tab. Preview strums a C, A minor, F and G on each before you choose it. Switching while the song plays never stops it.
  • A guitarist's hands. The fretting hand plays from the chord shapes a guitarist learns — open chords, the E- and A-shape barres, power chords, triads and shell voicings — each chord near the last, and a line in the position where it lies best, the fingertips pressed just behind the fret wire. The picking hand strums down and up in time, picks single notes, plays fingerstyle with the thumb and fingers, or rests the palm on the strings for the muted guitar. Only the hands are drawn.
  • Hammer-ons, pull-offs and slides. On a fast line the hand slurs where a guitarist would, and a slide carries the finger along the string across each fret, heard as one note gliding fret by fret.
  • Strings that respond. Each string is pressed down onto its fret where it is fretted and vibrates while its note rings, a strong stroke more than a soft one, a muted string barely at all, with the hands shown or hidden.
  • What the guitar plays, and Backing. Choose whether the guitar plays the Chords, the Melody or the Counter. With Backing off, the parts it does not play are silent and their sheets dimmed; with Backing on, they play on the Grand Piano. Your project keeps the choice.
  • TAB and chord diagrams. A guitar's sheet shows the notes on the staff over six-line TAB, chord diagrams at each change, strum arrows, slides and hammer-on and pull-off slurs, and the fingering when finger numbers are on. TAB is in the Export to PDF too.
  • The guitar's three views. Front faces the player, Top looks down the neck as the player does, and Side shows the picking hand and the neck in profile. The music stand stands beside the player, its sheets turning to face each view.
  • Fret wire. Settings has a new Instrument page: choose the electric's fret wire — vintage, narrow-tall, medium-jumbo or jumbo, in nickel-silver or stainless steel. It changes how the neck looks, never how it sounds.
  • Play the guitar yourself. With the hands hidden, press a string at a fret to pluck that note, held until you let go, its name shown on the neck. A MIDI key plays the nearest string and fret.
  • The guitar courses. Guitar (on the steel-string, the electric and the muted guitar) teaches shapes and TAB first: open chords and strumming, riffs with alternate picking, palm muting, power chords, barre chords, legato and the pentatonic scales, fingerpicking, and reading the treble clef. Classical Guitar (on the nylon guitar) reads from the staff with the TAB underneath: rest strokes, the bass strings and the thumb, keys and sharps, arpeggios, chords, free strokes, slurs and studies. Days 1 to 150 each, with Listen hearing your own guitar.
  • Dots on the neck. In a guitar lesson each coming note shows as a coloured dot on its string and fret, its finger's number inside, fading in before it sounds and lit on the beat; Wait mode shows the notes it waits for, and before you play, the whole shape of the exercise. Set how early the dots appear beside the tempo.

Changed

  • The top bar's keys. Listen, Shortcuts and the new Settings key (,) are now icons, each named when you point at it, so the top bar fits its width.

v0.12.02026-10-19

Live listening. The lessons hear a real instrument and a real voice now: switch Listen on for a course, and the piano course hears your acoustic piano through a microphone, the drum-kit course your acoustic kit's kick, snare and hi-hat, Rap & Flow your syllables on the beat, and ear training the answers you sing — judged on the notes and the time, as with MIDI keys. A tuner and a latency calibration sit in the Listen dialog. Everything is heard on your computer, only while the light is on, and nothing is recorded, kept or sent.

Added

  • The Listen dialog. Press L or the Listen key in the top bar: choose the input and its channel (or every channel, mixed), watch its level and its clip light, see the chord you strum named, and see at once when nothing is coming in, with where to allow the microphone on Windows and macOS. In a DAW, Handscore listens on its own sidechain input, Listen, its main outputs unchanged, and the dialog shows how to send a track to it in Ableton Live, FL Studio and Reaper.
  • The light. A light with the word Listening shows in the top bar, the lesson's bar and the flow tool exactly while the input is open, and only then. The input opens only while a lesson listens, the tuner shows or a calibration runs, and closes within seconds of leaving the lesson, closing the window or stopping.
  • Lessons that hear you. Each course that can listen has a Listen switch, off until you turn it on. The piano, reading and theory courses hear the notes and chords of an acoustic piano or any other instrument, in Try, In time, Perform and the questions, with the same checks and feedback as on MIDI, marked judged by ear: the notes and the time, never the touch, the pedal or how long a note was held. A chord counts by its notes' names, and what the stage plays is not mistaken for you.
  • The drum kit by microphone. The drum-kit course hears an acoustic kit's kick, snare and hi-hat, a kick with the hats as both, and judges every other piece by its time.
  • Rap & Flow and rhythm by voice. The rhythm and Rap & Flow drills time your syllables, claps or taps against the click, and the flow tool's Rap it times a rapped take against the syllables you placed, early or late, so you hear your pocket. Nothing is recognised: no word and no voice.
  • Ear training sung. Sing your answer: it counts in any octave, so a low voice can sing it an octave down.
  • The tuner. The note and how many cents sharp or flat, steady as the note rings, with A4 from 415 to 466 Hz and the guitar's and the bass's open strings named.
  • Latency calibration. Calibrate measures how long a sound takes to leave Handscore and come back in, looped back through a cable or your speakers into the microphone, or by playing along with the click, and keeps the number for that input on this computer, so what you play lands on time.

v0.11.02026-10-19

The drum machine and the drum kit. The Drums tab is a real drum machine now: put your own samples on its pads, layer them, shape each pad's sound, and program your beat step by step. Or switch the drums to a full acoustic kit and watch a drummer's hands and feet play your beat, every stroke where a real drummer would play it. One switch moves between them while the music plays, the same hits either way. Imported drum MIDI is read through a Drum Map you can set row by row, and the drum-kit course teaches the kit from your first stroke to full songs in Days 1 to 150.

Added

  • The drum machine. Sixteen pads, each with up to four layers by velocity or in turn, its own tune, pitch envelope, amp envelope, filter with its own envelope, gain, pan, choke group, mute and solo. Trim, fade, reverse, normalise and gain a sample without changing the file. Every change is one undo step and is heard at once.
  • Handscore's own drum kits. Four electronic kits — Sub, Floor, Neon and Wire — each with every sound the drums use, in three velocity layers and two round robins, and a style's drums start on the kit that suits it. Drive, Snap, Tone and Crush give the kit its character.
  • Your own samples on the pads. Drop a WAV, AIFF or FLAC from the file browser onto a pad, or add one with Add samples…. A file that is too long, too big, silent or not audio is refused with the reason. Handscore keeps its own copy, so the project plays even if the original moves, and your samples are listed in the Export to PDF.
  • The step sequencer. Every pad's row of steps for the loop's 4 or 8 bars: click a step, paint across, right-drag to erase and drag back to restore, set each hit's velocity, and swing the off-beats. The pattern Generate wrote and the takes you played in land in it, and every edit is heard, seen and dragged at once.
  • The drum kit. A professional's kit on stage — snare, kick, two rack toms and two floor toms, hi-hat, two crashes, ride, splash, china and cowbell — with the drummer's hands holding the sticks and feet on the pedals. Each hit is struck where a drummer would strike it, the right hand crossed over to the hi-hat, rudiments and fills hand to hand, the kick and the hi-hat under the feet, and a double pedal only when one foot cannot keep up. Brushes for jazz and ballads, rods for soft acoustic styles. Its own acoustic sound, with close, overhead and room microphones you can mix.
  • The drum machine or the drum kit. One switch on the Drums tab, on the Drums strip and beside the drums in the tray chooses which plays your drums. Switching while the song plays never stops it: the next hit sounds on the other, and the stage moves to it. Your project keeps the choice, and audio drags play what you chose.
  • The Drum Map. Drum MIDI from your DAW keeps every row as it came. Pick your DAW's layout (General MIDI, Ableton Drum Rack, FL Studio FPC, Logic Drummer, EZdrummer / Superior Drummer, Addictive Drums) or set each row to a kit piece, not on the kit, or the 808 bassline, and the drummer plays it at once. Save your own maps.
  • The drum-kit course. Days 1 to 150 in lesson mode on an electronic drum kit over MIDI: the grip and the stroke, single and double strokes, paradiddles, flams, drags and the rolls, the feet, the backbeat, ghost notes, the open hi-hat and the ride, shuffles and swing, brushes and rods, the double pedal, fills and forms, and grooves from rock and funk to one-drop, bossa nova and jazz, with the click dropping out in stages.
  • Finger drumming on the drum machine. The finger-drumming course now plays through the drum machine's pads, the exercise's pattern shown beside the stage.

v0.10.02026-10-17

Finger drumming. A pad controller takes the stage, its sixteen pads lighting up with every hit, and realistic hands play your loop's drums on it, finger by finger. Every loop now has drums of its own, printed on a drum sheet. Play your own in on any pad controller, layer on layer as the loop comes round, and keep the takes you like. The finger-drumming course teaches it from the first beat to full grooves.

Added

  • The pad controller on stage. Choose it in Select Mode under Instruments, or switch it on in the Drums tab: a 4×4 pad controller, with the hands playing the loop's drums on its pads, index fingers first, each hand keeping to its side. The other parts are still heard. Your project keeps the choice.
  • Every pad lights up as it is hit. Each kit piece has its own colour, and a hit's strength sets how bright it glows before it fades, like a real pad's light. With Reduce motion the colours stay and the fades go.
  • Drums on every loop. Generate now writes the loop's drums too, from MIDI Master's drum generators. With an 808 bassline, the kick and the 808 come as one pattern, and a new take of the bassline brings new drums with it.
  • The Drums tab. Sixteen pads on screen, after the Bassline tab, with its dot and its ×. Click them, play them from a MIDI controller, or switch on Keys play pads and play them from the computer keys (1 2 3 4, Q W E R, A S D F, Z X C V). Pick the Default layout (General MIDI notes 36 to 51 from the bottom-left pad) or the mirrored Performance layout. Learn maps your own controller: hit each pad when it is asked for.
  • The drum sheet. The Drums part is printed in standard drum notation, each piece on its own line or space, the hands' notes stemmed up and the feet's down. It stands on the desk with the other sheets (first, with the pad controller on stage) and has its number key like them, and it is in the tray with Solo and Mute and in the PDF. With finger numbers on, each note shows its hand and finger.
  • Play your own drums in. Record over the loop after a count-in: each time the loop comes round, the pass you played becomes a layer it plays on with, so you can add a kick, then a snare, then the hats. Undo pass takes the last one back, and Stop keeps the take, snapped to sixteenths if Quantise is on. Your project keeps every take with how tight it was; pick any one, or go back to the loop's own drums.
  • The finger-drumming course. Days 1 to 150 in lesson mode: the index fingers, then the thumbs and middle fingers, the backbeat, kick placements, swing, accents and ghost notes, fills, genre grooves, rudiments on one pad and across pads, triplets and the shuffle, linear grooves, with the click dropping out in stages. The next pads glow before they are due, a wrong pad flashes red, and each take says how far ahead of or behind the beat you were. A take played with the mouse or the computer keys is not marked on its accents.

Changed

  • Follow DAW is in DAWs only. The standalone has no Follow DAW switch. In a DAW, the Tempo chip stays at the DAW's tempo while Follow DAW is on; switch it off to set a tempo of your own.
  • The Arrangement label's keys. Undo, Redo, Mute, Solo and the zoom's − and + are small icons on the Arrangement label's line, which stays in view while the keys below it scroll. Mute and Solo light up while they are on, and each key has its own colour under the pointer.

v0.9.02026-10-17

Lessons beyond the piano. Nine new courses sit beside the piano course in lesson mode: reading real sheet music, rhythm, ear training, music theory for producers with the Nashville Number System, songwriting, lyric writing with a rap and flow track, song arrangement and mixing, each Day by Day from the first lesson to the end of its Elementary section. The piano course itself now runs on to about Grade 5. The creative courses are done in the Song view, on the lyric lane and in the Mixer, and checked by craft rules you can overrule; the Song view gains the songwriter's tools they use.

Added

  • Ten courses in lesson mode. A course picker above the map: Piano, Reading, Rhythm, Ear training, Theory for producers, Songwriting, Lyric Writing, Rap & Flow, Song Arrangement and Mixing, each keeping its own Days passed, its place and its clocks; Continue opens the course you were in. Lesson mode never changes your project: what a lesson moves is put back when you leave, and a project your DAW saves mid-lesson is saved as it was.
  • The piano course to about Grade 5. Days 401 to 1,300: every major and minor key with its proper fingering, scales a third and a sixth apart, seventh arpeggios, sixteenths, two-part pieces, sonatina, Romantic, jazz and duet pieces with the stage playing along, playing from memory, and checkpoints at about Grades 3, 4 and 5.
  • Reading. Hear it before you see it, the staff's landmarks, steps and skips, key signatures, ledger lines and chords, and fresh sight-reading every Day at your level, the sheet printed in the music's own key.
  • Rhythm. Counting, subdivision to sixteenths and triplets, syncopation, swing, compound and odd meters and polyrhythm on one key or two, with a click that drops out in stages until you keep time on your own.
  • Ear training. The stage plays a question and you answer on the keys, one question at a time: higher or lower, intervals, major or minor, chords and their inversions, cadences and progressions as bass lines, melodies and rhythms played back.
  • Theory for producers. Scale degrees, intervals, chords and their jobs, progressions, the modes, and the Nashville Number System — any generated song shown as a number chart, with split bars and pushes.
  • Songwriting, Lyric Writing, Rap & Flow and Song Arrangement. Day by Day from your first song to a dozen, in many genres, done in the Song view, the notebook and the lyric lane: each step's Check says what it found and where, and any target you broke on purpose can be marked intended.
  • Mixing. Gain staging, balance, panning, EQ, compression, Room and Echo, and loudness, on lesson loops in the Mixer; Check prints the loop and measures its peaks, its loudness and its true peak. Your project's own mix is never changed by a lesson.
  • Songwriting tools on the Song view. A song title; the lyric lane under the melody, each syllable under its note with its stress marked, the line ends coloured by rhyme and each section labelled with its rhyme scheme; common phrases flagged; a part switched off in one section and back on; each section's energy; named versions of the song and what changed since; the flow grid for rap; and a portfolio PDF — the lead sheet with its chord symbols and words, the lyric sheet and the craft report.

v0.8.02026-10-16

The Song view. Handscore now writes, builds and plays whole songs, not only loops: generate a complete song in any style's own form, or build one section by section on the Arrangement tab's timeline, and the hands and the sheets play it from the first bar to the last, the pages turning as they go. A song has its drums on a strip of their own, drags into your DAW whole, by section or by clip, and comes with a hit-readiness grade that shows, check by check, how it measures up to the songwriter's rules of thumb.

Added

  • The Arrangement tab. Press A: the song's timeline docks under the stage, so the piano, the hands and the sheets stay in view while you build, and the song plays while the tab is open (or soloed from any tab). Generate a style's whole song, pick one of its forms or its empty form, start a blank song, or drag a style from the left panel onto the timeline.
  • Build a song by hand. Add, insert, move, rename and resize sections; copy and paste clips, make a shared clip unique, fade a clip in and out, set how many bars it loops and lock the clips a new take keeps. Re-roll a section, loop one, or double-click a clip to hear it alone. Every change is one step of Undo.
  • Fill a cell your way. Send the loop on stage to a section, drop a .mid or an audio file on a cell from the new file browser (audio is turned into notes), or play a take into a cell on your MIDI keyboard, quantised if you like.
  • Edit a clip's notes in the song. A clip's ✎ opens its notes in the part editor under the timeline: pick, move, lengthen, draw, copy, paste and delete notes in every section that shares the clip, or make it unique to change one alone. Play notes in on your MIDI keyboard from the bar you choose.
  • Sheets and hands across the whole song. Every sheet marks each section after a double bar line and shows a part's rest as one bar with its count; past a page's middle its top half already turns to the next page, and Go to a section turns every sheet there. The hands plan their moves across sections, and a hand with nothing to play lifts off the keys and rests.
  • The Drums row. A song's drums play on the style's drum machine, on a strip of their own in the Mixer. In Playable voicing they keep only what two hands and two feet could play; Raw keeps every hit.
  • A song into your DAW. Drag the whole song with its sections as markers, one section alone or one clip, as MIDI or as audio (up to 15 minutes). Export MIDI and Export to PDF work from the Song view too.
  • Re-Dream the whole song. Three new versions of the song in its own form, key, tempo and metre, your locked clips kept; keep the one you like.
  • The hit-readiness grade. A letter and a score out of 100 from twelve checks on the song's form, its parts and its words: how often the hook returns and how early it arrives, the intro's length, contrast between sections, the tempo for the style, the melody's range and shape, and how the lyrics fit, rhyme and repeat. Each check says what it found and what it aims for, so the grade teaches; type the melody's words in its box to grade them too. It is on the PDF's cover as well.

v0.7.02026-10-16

The mixer. Every part now plays through a channel strip of its own — fader, pan, EQ, room, echo, heat, a compressor and a Granular knob — into a master, with meters, mute and solo, all kept with your project. Drag any part, or the whole mix, into your DAW as audio exactly as you hear it. Shape each part's sound with the envelope editor, and play any part on one of your own VST3 or CLAP instruments.

Added

  • The Mixer. Press M: every part's strip side by side and the master — a printed fader that follows your pointer, an LED meter with its clip light, the knob grid (Low, Mid, High, Room, Echo, Heat), a compressor with its gain-reduction light, pan, solo and mute. Every gesture is heard while it moves and is one undo step when you let go.
  • Mute and solo that glide. A part fades out and back in over 30 ms instead of cutting, and solo hears only the parts soloed — Ctrl+click adds one.
  • The Granular knob. On every strip and on the master: from nothing at 0 % to a cloud of grains, echoes and a wash at 100 %, a sound that turns any part ambient. Seeded by the project, so a print is the same twice.
  • Audio drags. Each part's audio grip drags that part as a 24-bit WAV, printed through its strip at the song's tempo; the full mix drags as you hear it and names the parts it leaves out.
  • MIDI drags that carry the mix. A part's fader and pan go into the file as CC 7 and CC 10, and the grip lists what MIDI cannot carry.
  • The envelope editor. Each generator's volume, filter, pitch, pan and Granular envelopes, every one off until you switch it on: points to drag, segments to bend, a held loop, tempo-locked times, velocity and key tracking, retrigger or legato, an A/B morph, starting shapes, copy to another part, undo and redo, and a live dot for each note riding the curve.
  • Your own instruments. Each part can play one of your own VST3 or CLAP instruments instead of Handscore's: choose it on the part's tab or strip from the instruments a scan finds, open its window from there, and your project keeps the plugin and its sound. Each plugin runs in a process of its own, so one that crashes stops only itself, and its latency is compensated. The hands stay on Handscore's instrument, and that part drags as MIDI.

v0.6.02026-10-16

Lessons. Handscore now teaches the piano, Day by Day, from your very first notes to about Grade 2: 400 Days in two sections, Beginner and Elementary, each a guided session of its own. The hands show you each exercise from any view, then the course listens as you play on your MIDI keyboard and moves on only when it is right. Switch on Read Aloud and your computer's own voice reads the lesson to you, each word lit as it is spoken.

Added

  • Lessons in the studio. Select Mode → Lessons opens the piano course: the course map and the Day's steps on the left, the stage in the middle, the lesson's text under it. Exit goes back to the song you left.
  • 400 Days of piano. Beginner (Days 1–90) and Elementary (Days 91–400): sitting and hand shape, the five-finger positions, reading the grand staff, rhythm, chords, the pedal, scales and arpeggios, hands together and pieces to play, with a review Day every week and earlier work brought back when it is due.
  • Listen and check, three ways. Try: the keys to play light up and the music waits for them. In time: in tempo after a count-in, every note checked against the beat. Perform: play it through, with a summary at the end.
  • Feedback like a teacher's. Each take names the bar and the beat and gives the fix — a note missed, early or late, joined or detached, the pedal — and says what went well.
  • Practice tools. Loop a few bars, play one hand while the stage plays the other, climb the tempo ladder (a step up after three clean takes, a step back after three misses) and watch slow demonstrations in any view with the finger numbers shown. The key names fade as you learn them.
  • Read Aloud. Off until you switch it on, and remembered. Your computer's voice reads the lesson, the word it is saying lit like a karaoke screen and the text scrolling to keep it in view. Play and pause with Space, stop, hold ⏪ or ⏩ to run back or ahead, set the pace from half speed to double and choose the voice; a clock shows the reading time gone and left.
  • Your progress, kept. The Days you have passed, the time spent in each lesson and in all of them, and the days you practised, in days, hours, minutes and seconds — on this computer, in the standalone and every DAW. Continue opens exactly where you stopped: the Day, the step and the word.

v0.5.02026-10-15

The overhead and side views. Watch the hands from the front, from straight above or from the side, and glide from one view to another while the music plays — the keys, the hands and the lit notes never miss a beat. From above, a grand closes its lid and the sheets lie flat on it, ready to read; from the side, the shape of the hands shows: how each finger curves and how far each key goes down.

Added

  • Three views: Front, Top and Side. Turn the view knob in the stage's top-right corner, or press V for the next view and Shift+V for the one before. Your project remembers the view.
  • A seamless switch. The camera glides from one view to the next without ever passing through the piano, and the music plays on through the move: every key, hand and lit note is exactly where it would be with the camera standing still.
  • Top: the keys and the hands from above. A grand folds its prop, lays its music desk flat and closes its lid; the sheets lift off the desk, wait over the keys and lie down on the closed lid side by side, reading upright from where you sit. An upright's sheets lie on its top lid.
  • Side: the hands in profile. The camera looks along the keyboard from the treble end, at the height of the keys — or from the bass end, with From the left under the view knob — and the sheets turn on the desk toward it so they stay readable.
  • The hands from every side. They cast their shadows on the keys they play, and from above no fingertip ever sinks into a key or the case.
  • A sheet close up in any view. Click it, or press 1 to 5; Esc goes back to the view.
  • Smooth in every view. After each change of view or piano, Handscore checks again how smoothly the stage draws, and if it has slowed down, lowers the quality and says so.

v0.4.02026-10-15

Generate, read and drag. Search any of 73 genres, 1,507 artists or 833 producers, press Generate, and four parts — chords, melody, counter-melody and bassline — are written in that style and played by the hands on the piano. Each part is printed as real sheet music on the piano's music desk, beside a full score, every note lighting as it sounds. Drag a part, or the whole song, straight into your DAW, edit any note in its own piano roll, and export the MIDI or a PDF of the sheets with your credits.

Added

  • Generate in any style. Search a genre, an artist or a producer in the panel on the left (its tab on the app's edge shows and hides it) and press Generate, or G. Chips beside it set the key, the scale (41 of them), the mood, the tempo (20 to 999 BPM; drag up or down on the number), the complexity and 4 or 8 bars, and each reads what the song was made in. Follow DAW writes at your project's tempo.
  • Four parts, played by the hands. Chords, melody, counter-melody and bassline, voiced for two hands (Playable) or exactly as written (Raw). Every generation fills its 4 or 8 bars, and switching 4 to 8 repeats the first four.
  • Real sheet music. Every part printed as a page on the music desk, with its title and page number, and a full score of all the parts beside them. Every note head lights as it sounds under a moving playhead, and a long part turns its pages as it plays. Click a sheet, or press 1 to 5, to bring it close; Esc goes back.
  • The sheet tray. Under the stage, each part with Solo, Mute, Lock and a new take. A locked part stays when you generate again.
  • Drag into your DAW. Drag one part, or the whole song with Drag all, from its sheet into your DAW as MIDI.
  • Export. Save the MIDI of a part or the whole song, with every note's velocity, each part's level and pan, the tempo and the time signature.
  • Export to PDF. A PDF of the sheets with a cover page: the title, tempo, key and scale, length, bars, the notes in total, per part and per page, and the instruments. Add credits — writers, vocalists, producers, the samples you used and where they came from, the record label — with names suggested as you type.
  • A tab for each part. Stage, Melody, Chords, Counter and Bassline along the top, each part's tab with its own piano roll and a mute dot, Solo and a × that clears it.
  • Edit the notes. Select, move, lengthen, delete, copy, paste and clone notes in any part's roll; right-click and drag to take notes away and back. Record from a MIDI keyboard into the roll after a count-in of one or two bars. Each part has its own gain and pan.
  • Scales on the rolls. Fold a roll to the song's scale or to the rows that hold notes; the scale's rows are tinted and a note outside it is marked.
  • One-note chords. Switch them on and one key plays a chord of 3, 4 or 5 notes in the song's key, from the mouse, the computer keyboard or a MIDI keyboard.
  • Ghost notes. As you draw, the next note or chord is suggested faintly; Tab keeps it, a click keeps one, and drawing elsewhere clears it.
  • Lyrics. Type words under the melody or the counter-melody, the syllables marked, and they are printed on the sheet and lit as they are sung. A counter beside each line shows whether it fits its bar. As you type, the rest of the word is suggested (Tab takes it) and a misspelling is corrected, unless you tap the checkmark or press Shift+Tab to keep your word.
  • Take, Dream 1–3 and Re-Dream. Three new versions of any part beside the one you have; pick one and keep it. A classic's Re-Dream flips it into a new song of your own.
  • Seeds. Every generation shows its seed and each part's take seed, with Copy. Paste a song's seed to make that song again, or a part's take seed to bring that take back.
  • History. Back and Forward through every generation you have made, each brought back exactly, kept across restarts. A new generation never repeats an old one.
  • Classics stay yours to learn, never to take. Only a classic's chords may leave it. A generated song whose melody, counter-melody or bassline matches a well-known tune keeps that part in, and says why.
  • The Keyboard Shortcuts dialog. Press ? or its key in the top bar to see every shortcut.
  • Your project remembers. Saving your DAW project keeps the song, its edits, its mix, its words, the piano, the view and the credits, and reopening brings them back as they were.

Changed

  • The timeline's preview is larger, and shows the words being sung at that moment.

v0.3.02026-10-14

The realistic hands. A pair of hands now plays the piano on Handscore's stage the way a pianist would — each finger chosen for the passage, the thumb passing under on a scale, a hand lifting up and over the other when the music crosses. Sixty-two public-domain classics are ready to learn, from Mary Had a Little Lamb to Silent Night and The Star-Spangled Banner, and a transport under the stage lets you pause, rewind, fast-forward or drag to any moment, hearing the notes as you go.

Added

  • Realistic hands at the keyboard. Two hands, ending at the wrist, play every note on all four pianos: each finger chosen as a pianist would choose it, the thumb passing under on scales, wide chords reached, the thumb lying on its side and gently bent, and a hand lifting up and over the other to cross. They rest on the keys, ready, when nothing plays.
  • Fingering worked out before a note sounds. Handscore decides which hand plays each note and with which finger before the music plays, so the hands arrive as the keys go down rather than after.
  • Select Mode. The button at the top left (or P) now reads Select Mode and opens your choices by category: Instruments, Classics, Passages, and Lessons (coming in a later version).
  • Sixty-two classics to learn. Nursery songs, Christmas songs and carols, folk songs, spirituals and anthems, classical themes and piano favourites — Chopsticks, Jingle Bells, Silent Night, Ode to Joy, Für Elise, The Entertainer, The Star-Spangled Banner and more — each arranged for two hands, the melody in the right and the chords in the left. Search them by title or composer. Every one is in the public domain.
  • Passages to watch. A scale, a ii–V–I, an arpeggio and a crossing, each a quick look at the hands at work.
  • The transport. Play, pause, stop, rewind and fast-forward under the stage, with a timeline showing the song's time and its bars. Hover over the timeline to see the hands at that moment in a small preview. Drag along it, or hold rewind or fast-forward, and you hear every note you pass, in either direction.
  • A metronome. Switch it on and a click marks each beat, the first of every bar higher, while the dots beside its M show the beat.
  • Space plays and pauses.

Changed

  • The instrument button is now Select Mode, and the piano you choose is marked Current among the Instruments.

v0.2.02026-10-14

The real pianos. Four pianos now stand on Handscore's stage — a concert grand, a parlor grand, a studio upright and an old saloon upright — each lit like a studio and each with its own voice. Click a key or play from your DAW and the piano plays: the key goes down, its name fades, and the sustain pedal holds what you let go. A picker lets you hear a piano before you choose it.

Added

  • Four real pianos. Grand Piano, Acoustic Piano, Upright Piano and Honky-Tonk Piano, each with its own shape, finish and sound, in both themes.
  • Play the keys. Click a key to hear it, or slide across the keys to play each in turn. MIDI from your DAW plays the piano and moves its keys, and the sustain pedal holds the notes you let go until you lift it.
  • Keys that move like a piano's. Each key goes down on its balance rail, faster the harder it is played, and its name fades while it is down and comes back when it rises.

  • Quality that fits your computer. Handscore measures how fast your computer draws the stage and settles on the quality it can hold smoothly.

Fixed

  • Keys keep up on a busy computer. A key played while the computer was slow to draw could stay up, or its name stay half-faded; it now always ends where your playing left it.

v0.1.02026-10-12

The foundation. Freally Handscore loads as a CLAP and VST3 plugin and as a standalone app, asks you to accept its licence, and opens on its stage: an 88-key keyboard at a real piano's measurements, with every key named on its front. Nothing is generated yet — that arrives with the real pianos and the hands.

Added

  • The plugin and the standalone app. CLAP and VST3 on Windows (the AU is built on a Mac), and a standalone app that wears Handscore's icon in its title bar and taskbar.
  • The licence agreement comes first. Until you agree, Handscore does nothing. Your answer is kept on your computer and shared by every open copy, in every DAW and in the standalone app.
  • The stage. An 88-key keyboard, A0 to C8, at a real piano's measurements, filling the width of the window. Every key's name is on its front — white keys in dark ink, black keys in white — and hides while the key is down.
  • Light and dark. The switch at the top right; Handscore remembers your choice.
  • Eighteen languages. Handscore starts in your system's language.
  • A 3D view that says when it cannot draw. A window without WebGL 2 explains how to turn it on instead of showing nothing.
  • Crashes are kept, never sent. If Handscore ever stops unexpectedly, a short report is kept on your computer for the next launch. Nothing leaves your computer unless you choose to report it.
