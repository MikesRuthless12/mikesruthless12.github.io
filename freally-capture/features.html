Inside

What version 1.2.1 does

Scenes and sources on the GPU

Displays, windows, games, webcams and capture cards, web pages, media, playlists, images, text, timers and test signals, composed live on the graphics card. Nested scenes, groups, shared sources, a vertical 9:16 canvas, and undo for every edit.

Filters and transitions

Chroma key, color correction, LUTs, masks, a family of blurs, 3D tilt and your own WGSL shaders, on each source. Cut, fade, slide, stinger, luma wipe and a move transition that glides items from scene to scene.

Studio Mode and monitoring

Stage the next shot in the preview and send it to the program when it is ready. A multiview, fullscreen projectors on any display, downstream keyers over every scene, and a stats dock with real numbers.

Multistream, straight from your machine

Go live on several platforms at once over RTMP, RTMPS, SRT or WHIP, with no relay service in between. Each target reconnects on its own, so one failing never takes down the rest.

Recording that survives

Hardware encoders, with x264 as a fallback, to MP4, MKV, MOV or WebM, or Capture's own lossless format; up to six audio tracks; a clean file for each source; recordings that survive a crash, and a repair on the next launch.

A broadcast audio console

Noise suppression, gate, compressor, limiter, parametric EQ and de-esser on every strip, with ducking, an auto-mixer, loudness normalization, a soundboard and podcast recording. Classic signal processing, with no AI.

Instant replay

A rolling replay buffer that saves the last moments with one key, and a replay source that rolls them back into the show at full, half or quarter speed. A clip trimmer exports without re-encoding when it can.

Remote guests from a browser

Up to eight guests join from a link, in a web browser, with nothing to install. A green room, private cues, each guest's connection at a glance, and an automatic grid with nameplates.

Broadcast safety

A pre-flight before going live, alarms for silence, clipping, a frozen picture and a filling disk, encoder failover mid-show, a panic button, a rehearsal mode that sends nothing, and a quit that finishes everything in order.

Automation and control

Rules, macros and variables; MIDI and OSC controllers; a touch panel and tally lights on your phone; PTZ cameras; a show rundown; a WebSocket API for Stream Deck; Lua scripts and plugins. All off by default.

Virtual camera, browser source, game capture

Your program as a webcam for video-call apps; any web page rendered as a source; and exclusive-fullscreen games through a hook you approve game by game. Windows first, with the gaps stated below.

Local, private, no AI

No account, no telemetry and no cloud service of ours. Your recordings and stream keys stay on your computer, and no filter uses a model: every one is classic signal processing.

The short version

What Freally Capture has that they don't: it is the only desktop app here that runs on Linux as well as Windows and macOS, and the only one that is free. Four of the six need an account and four have AI features; Capture has neither, and it multistreams straight from your computer, where Streamlabs and Restream relay your stream through their own servers.

Where they win

Where they win: Wirecast Pro records up to 8 audio tracks to Capture's 6, and Streamlabs Ultra takes up to 11 guests or cameras and Ecamm up to 10, to Capture's 8. Streamlabs, XSplit, vMix, Wirecast and Ecamm all have a virtual camera; Capture's works on Windows only in 1.2.1 and is not yet code-signed.

What about OBS Studio?

OBS Studio is free and open source too, runs on all three systems, and has SRT, WHIP, a virtual camera, a replay buffer and six audio tracks. Streaming to several platforms at once needs a third-party plugin there; in Capture it is built in, along with guests who join from a browser.

Capture's price is today's: it is free today, and future versions may be sold. The other products change too; this table is as of 2 October 2026.

Principles

Why it is built the way it is

It stays on your machine

A streaming tool should never become "connect your channel". There is no account to make and no relay of ours in the middle: your stream goes from your computer to the platforms you chose.

Its own engine

The compositor, the audio engine and the lossless recording format are Capture's own. The codecs the platforms require come from ffmpeg, downloaded the first time you need it rather than bundled.

Classic signal processing

Noise suppression, keying and tone-mapping are formulas, not models. The same input and the same settings give the same result, and nothing is downloaded to make them work.

Off until you turn it on

Every network listener, every automation and every risky capture is off by default. Game capture asks you for each game, and a shared scene collection can never carry that permission.

Honest about its gaps

When a feature works only on one system, is not code-signed yet, or still owes a live test, the app, the changelog and this page say so.

See every rung of the road

What 1.2.1 does not do

  • The virtual camera works on Windows only; the macOS and Linux cameras are not in this release.
  • Game capture works on Windows only and copies Direct3D 11 games; HDR and Direct3D 12 games fall back to window capture.
  • The browser source shows a page's picture but not its sound yet.
  • The virtual camera's system component and the browser runtime are not code-signed yet, and two live tests are still owed: the camera inside a real video call, and game capture in a protected game.
  • Cursor effects and the keyboard and mouse input overlay work on Windows only.
  • It has no AI features — no AI background removal, captions or highlight clips — by design.
