Inside

What it does

A room with no server behind it

Send a room code to whoever you are working with and up to three other producers can join your session: their cursors on your timeline, chat, voice, camera tiles and screen share. Peer to peer, with no account and no server in the middle.

Your undo is yours

Ctrl+Z takes back your last change and never somebody else's, even while their edits are arriving. The edit log behind it is also the room's sync, Session Rewind and the Split Sheet.

Takeover

Hand one track to a collaborator and take it back with one key. While they hold it they are the only writer for it — including you — and that is enforced where edits are checked, not only in the interface.

Kumite

A beat battle mode in which each turn owns its own bars. The countdown is a countdown; the bars are the rule, so two computers can never disagree about whose edit was legal.

The Split Sheet

Who wrote what, derived from the edit log, with work anybody undid netted out. A starting point for a conversation about credit, not a legal document — and it says so.

Takes travel; samples never do

A take recorded in the room lands on everybody's timeline. A sample you bought is never sent: the room sends the reference, and a collaborator without the pack is told which samples they need.

The arrangement

Unlimited audio and MIDI tracks, clips drawn from what they are, automation on every fader, pan and device control, comping, groups, markers, and tempo and time-signature maps. A pattern placed ten times is one pattern: edit it once and every placement changes, until you make one unique.

The Cut and Session Rewind

Draw a blade stroke across the arrangement and it splits every clip it crosses, the angle of the stroke setting the crossfade. Scrub the session's history like a video, and branch from any point in it.

The piano roll

Around ninety scales with highlighting, snapping and folding, ghost notes from any other clip, a velocity lane with a handle per note, lanes for how often each note plays and how far its velocity wanders, and a keyboard where one key can play a whole chord.

Recording and comping

Audio recording and a hardware MIDI keyboard on Windows, macOS and Linux. Takes stack under each other, you choose the parts you want, and the takes you did not choose are never deleted. Audio is written to disk as it arrives, so a crash costs the last block rather than the take.

Your plugins, each in a process of its own

VST3 and CLAP. A plugin that crashes greys out its device and leaves the session running. Delay compensation is graph-wide and proved by a null test, and a plugin this machine does not have becomes a named placeholder, so a project opened somewhere else stays in time.

Its own instruments and effects

Sixty-nine devices, among them Freally Secretarium and its 1 648 instruments. Each panel is drawn from the device's own description, so the number on a knob is the number the audio is using, and a device's settings copy as one line you can paste into a chat.

Devices that read the arrangement

Six devices do what a plugin cannot, because a plugin cannot see the song it is in: a compressor already holding the chorus when it arrives, a gate that opens for a hit before it happens, a delay that empties into the bar line. None of the six adds latency.

The mixer

A strip per track with a fader, pan, mute, solo, the device rack and a meter drawn from the audio the engine actually produced. The Masking Radar names two tracks fighting for the same band and the frequency; the loudness panel shows integrated and short-term LUFS, the range and true peak against what each streaming service targets.

The Lyric Lane

Lyrics as a lane on the timeline, locked to the playhead and split into syllables, imported from .txt or .lrc and copied into the project — in type meant to be sung from across the room.

Session cards and Session Timelapse

One shortcut renders a 1200×630 picture of the session to post. Session Timelapse rebuilds the whole session from its edit log as an animated picture with chapters — even a session nobody thought to record.

Your session opens without Oscillate

The companion folder writes stems and MIDI that any other DAW can open, lined up at bar 1, in the format, sample rate and bit depth you choose.

The family look

Knobs, faders and buttons rendered in Blender, five skins in light and dark beside both themes, short interface sounds you can switch off, and eighteen languages with real right-to-left — the same look in every Freally app.

Nothing phones home

No account and no telemetry. It uses the network on three occasions only: a room you opened or joined, the address lookup while that room is set up, and an update check that is off until you switch it on. Crash reports stay on your disk unless you choose to send one.

No AI, enforced by the build

No model, no inference, nothing generative. A build gate walks the dependency graph and fails on any inference runtime or model file, so it is a property of the build rather than a sentence on a website.

Principles

Why it is built the way it is

No server, so nothing to switch off

A room code is the connection offer itself. With no server there is nothing to keep running, nothing to pay for and nothing anybody can turn off — which is also why a room holds four producers and not forty: every peer sends to every other peer.

One log, four uses

Every edit is an operation in one log. Undo, the room's sync, Session Rewind and the Split Sheet are four readings of it, which is why your undo can be yours alone and why credit can be derived rather than typed in.

A crash is one device, not the session

Every hosted plugin runs in a process of its own, and the application itself cannot even load one. A plugin that falls over greys out its device, and the transport keeps playing.

The number you see is the number in the audio

Meters are drawn from what the engine produced, a knob's readout from the value the DSP is using, and a measurement that fell behind says so. Nothing on screen is a prediction.

It stays on your machine

Your projects, audio and MIDI leave your computer only when you are in a room with somebody — and then they go straight to the people in it, not through anybody's cloud.

What 1.0 will not do

  • Host AU or VST2 plugins. It hosts VST3 and CLAP.
  • Use ASIO. On Windows the low-latency path is WASAPI exclusive, and Oscillate tells you which mode you actually got.
  • Hold more than four producers in a room. The fifth connection is where an ordinary home upload gives out.
  • Relay a connection that will not form. Roughly one in ten will not without a relay in the middle; Oscillate names the cause, and Settings takes your own relay.
  • Share a screen from macOS. Screen share is Windows and Linux; on macOS the button is absent and says why.
  • Carry pitch bend, the mod wheel or pressure from a hardware keyboard. Notes, velocity and the sustain pedal are carried.

No beta on this page, no preview build, no early access. Until 1.0.0 the honest answer to “where do I get it” is not yet.
