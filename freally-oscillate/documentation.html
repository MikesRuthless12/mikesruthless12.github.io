    Getting started

    Oscillate installs like any other desktop application and runs offline. The first time you open it you are shown the agreement; you have to accept it before the application opens, and you can reopen it at any time from Settings ▸ About ▸ View the agreement.

    After that you are in a session called New Project. Nothing is saved anywhere until you save it, except the crash journal — which records every change as you make it so that a crash costs you nothing rather than costing you the afternoon.

    Press Space to start and stop. Drag a sound from the File Explorer onto a track, or double-click an empty part of a MIDI track to make a clip and start writing.

    The window and its panels

    The window is borderless and Oscillate draws its own minimise, maximise and close. It opens clean: the transport, the Pattern Rack docked above the arrangement, and the arrangement. Every panel is shown and hidden by its key in the titlebar, by its item in the View menu, or with a key:

    Key Panel What it is
    Ctrl+1 File Explorer Your sounds, your plugins and Oscillate's own devices, with a preview.
    Ctrl+2 Pattern Rack Patterns you build row by row, docked above the arrangement with a splitter between them.
    Ctrl+3 Editor The piano roll, the drum grid, automation and the waveform editor.
    Ctrl+4 Mixer Faders, meters, the Masking Radar and the loudness panel.
    Ctrl+5 Room Room codes, the roster, chat, voice, cameras and screen share.
    Ctrl+6 Stage Hands that play a MIDI track on a 3D instrument, in time with the transport.
    Ctrl+7 Sheets Any MIDI track as sheet music, following the playhead.
    Ctrl+8 Lessons Fifteen courses, learned a day at a time: each lesson shows you how, then listens as you play.
    Ctrl+9 Final check A master for each destination, checked as a phone and a car play it, section by section and against a delivery spec.

    On macOS every Ctrl below is Cmd.

    Panels in windows of their own

    The Mixer, the File Explorer, the Editor, the Room and the Final check live in windows of their own, hidden until you open them. A panel window's ✕ hides it; its ⇲ docks the panel into the main window, showing; and a docked panel's ⇱ pops it out into its window again. The Pattern Rack docks and pops out the same way. Where each panel lives is remembered on this computer, and a panel window keeps working on a second monitor. The Stage, the Sheets and the Lessons always stay in the main window.

    A panel window first opens beside the main window, cascaded, and after that where you left it, or beside the main window again if that display is gone. The Editor's window first opens 1360 × 1014, room for the piano roll's toolbar, two octaves, the automation lane and two take lanes; docked, the editor keeps 260 pixels and scrolls.

    A panel window that was open when Oscillate closed opens again at the next launch, where you left it, once the main window shows and the agreement has been accepted; one whose display is gone comes back beside the main window. A window you closed stays closed. What was open is noted as you open and close windows, not only when you quit, so a crash keeps it too.

    An install from before 0.10.0 moves to this layout once, on its first launch, and keeps its panel sizes. While the walkthrough (Help ▸ Tutorial) runs, the File Explorer and the Editor come into the main window so it can point at them, and go back to their windows when it ends.

    Drag the divider between two panels to resize them. Below 1280 pixels wide the side panels collapse on their own, because the arrangement stops being usable before they do.

    The arrangement

    A timeline with a bar-and-beat ruler, a grid that follows the zoom, track headers down the side, and clips drawn from what they actually are — a real waveform for audio, real notes for a pattern.

    Editing clips

    • Drag a clip to move it; hold Alt while dragging to copy it.
    • Drag either end to trim; drag the top corner to fade.
    • Drag the loop handle on the right edge to repeat it.
    • Ctrl+Z takes back the whole gesture — however many clips it touched, one press.

    The Cut

    Press B for the blade and drag a stroke across the arrangement. Every clip the stroke crosses is split where it crossed, and the angle of the stroke sets how long the crossfade is: a vertical stroke is a hard cut, a shallow one is a long blend. Press V to go back to the pointer.

    Turning a clip into the other kind

    Right-click a MIDI clip and choose Render to audio; right-click an audio clip and choose Convert to MIDI. Either way the result lands beside the original rather than over it, and one press of undo puts things back.

    A pattern is rendered through whatever is on that track: the instrument in its slot and then every audio effect after it. A device you switched off is not in the render. The track's own fader and pan deliberately are not either — the new clip plays through them, so printing them would apply them twice.

    Converting audio to MIDI is imperfect and says so: it tells you how many hits it could not read a pitch from. A drum loop converts to nothing at all, which is the honest answer.

    Locking a track

    The padlock on an audio or MIDI track's header, on its mixer strip and on its chip in the Sheets tray locks the track. While it is locked, every change to its notes and clips is refused with a sentence that names the track and says to unlock it, wherever the change comes from: the piano roll, the drum grid, the Pattern Rack, the arrangement, a dropped file, Record or undo. The track cannot be deleted while it is locked either. A take that was already recording when the lock came still lands when you stop. In a room the lock reaches everyone, and from then on each person's own changes to the track are refused.

    Its mix stays free: its name, colour, fader, pan, mute, solo, devices and routing all still change. The lock is saved with the project, takes one press of undo, and reaches everyone in a room.

    The piano roll

    Double-click a MIDI clip to open it. Draw notes with the pencil (P), or double-click in the grid. The keyboard down the left side plays the instrument on the track, and one key can be set to play a whole chord.

    Scales

    Around ninety scales. Choosing one highlights its notes, and Ctrl+K moves whatever is selected onto it. Folding hides every row that is not in the scale, which turns a chromatic grid into one you can read.

    The note operations

    Key What it does
    Ctrl+U Quantize — snap the selected notes to the grid
    Ctrl+Shift+U Humanize — push them back off it, by a little
    Ctrl+L Legato — stretch each note to meet the next
    Ctrl+R Reverse the order of the selected notes
    Ctrl+K Move them onto the clip's scale
    Ctrl+D Duplicate in place
    Shift+D Duplicate after — the usual way to build a part
    ← → Nudge by one grid division
    ↑ ↓ Move a semitone; with Shift, an octave

    The velocity lane under the grid has a handle per note. Ghost notes from any other clip can be drawn behind the one you are editing, which is how you write a bassline against a chord part without switching back and forth.

    The drum grid

    The same clip seen as one row per drum instead of one row per pitch, laid out as a step sequencer. With Used voices only on, only the drums the clip plays get a row. Everything in the piano roll's table above works here too — it is the same notes underneath — and As a piano roll takes you back to the roll.

    Steps

    Click a step to set or clear a hit. Drag along a row to fill the steps, or to clear them if the first one you pressed was set. Right-drag a box to take the hits inside it away, and drag back to put them back; a right-click on its own takes away the hit under it. Each drag is one press of undo.

    Each hit is drawn as a bar as tall as its velocity, and a hit played early or late of its step is marked so. Click a row's name and its velocities appear in a lane under the grid: drag across the bars to redraw them.

    Swing, from 50 to 75 %, pushes the off-beat sixteenths later. A hit played off the grid stays where it was played.

    Each row has M to mute it and S to solo it while the rest of the kit plays on, and a Choke group: a drum silences every other drum in its group, the way a closed hi-hat cuts an open one. On a Drum Machine track the rows are named by its pads, and a row's choke group is the pad's own.

    The Drum Map

    Drums written for another kit's layout can play the right pieces of yours. Press Drum Map and choose what the part is Read as: General MIDI, Ableton Drum Rack, FL Studio FPC, Logic Drummer, EZdrummer / Superior Drummer or Addictive Drums. Every note the part holds gets a row with its hits and what it Plays: a piece of your kit, the 808 / bassline, or Not on the kit. A row you change is marked, and Reset puts it back.

    The notes themselves never change. Switch the map off and the part plays exactly as it came; while it is on, playback, export, a clip dragged out and the stage all follow it.

    Your pad controller

    In Settings ▸ MIDI ▸ Pad controller, press Learn and hit each of the 16 pads when asked, counting from the bottom-left; Esc cancels. From then on, while the armed track plays a Drum Machine, your controller's pads play its pads. Until you teach it, the common layout is used: notes 36–51 from the bottom-left, which Forget goes back to.

    Layout chooses which drum each pad plays, the default or a mirrored Performance layout, and Fingers chooses the fingers the stage plays a pad part with, from one index finger to every finger.

    The waveform editor

    Double-click an audio clip to open it in the Editor: its waveform, and under it what can be done to the sound. None of it changes your file.

    Moving the pitch

    Drag across the waveform to choose a span, set Semitones (−12 to +12) and Cents (−50 to +50), and press Move pitch. The span moves with its formants kept, so a voice moved up a tone still sounds like the same voice. The moves are drawn as a bar under the waveform, and Clear pitch moves takes them all off in one press. Each move is one undo step.

    Oscillate works the moved sound out away from the audio thread, with a progress bar, and keeps it in its data folder; until it is ready the clip plays as it was. A trim never slides a move, every export carries it, and in a room the others make the same sound from the same numbers.

    Fitting a clip to the tempo

    Fit to tempo makes a clip follow the project's tempo once Material is this many bars says how long it is. Repitch plays it faster or slower, like tape, so its pitch moves with the tempo; Stretch (keeps the pitch) changes its length and keeps its pitch, on drums and chords as well as a single voice. The stretched sound is made the same way as a pitch move. A reversed or repeating clip cannot follow the tempo yet, and says so.

    A moved or stretched clip is still drawn as its file: the waveform shows the recording, not the sound that plays.

    The Pattern Rack

    The rack sits docked above the arrangement, with a splitter between them, from the first time Oscillate opens. Ctrl+2, its key in the titlebar or View ▸ Pattern Rack shows and hides it. It works the way FL Studio's does: build a part here, hear it on its own, and place it in the song when it is ready.

    Patterns and rows

    Press New pattern to make one. The + at the bottom left adds a row: choose an instrument from the browser's shelves, searching them by name, and it makes a new MIDI track with that instrument and gives it a row, all in one undo step. The same list adds a track already in the song.

    A row is a track: its name and its Devices are the track's, so it plays through that track's instrument and effects. Every MIDI row has a part of its own from the start; the list beside it links it to another part instead, or starts a New empty part.

    A row on one line

    Each row reads across one line, the way a channel does in FL Studio's rack: its light, lit while the track plays and dark while it is muted; its pan and volume knobs; where it plays into, the master or a group; its name; and S, its solo (Ctrl-click to solo it beside the others). The knobs are the track's own mixer pan and fader, so turning one moves the mixer strip, and the mixer moves the knob. Edit, Place, Move up and Remove are small keys at the row's end.

    Edit opens a row's part in the piano roll, or in the drum grid for a drum track. Place puts it on its track at the playhead. Place pattern puts every row on its own track at the playhead in one undo step, and every placement stays linked to its part: edit the pattern and each placement changes with it.

    Steps in the rack

    A row's steps are right there in the rack, in groups of four shaded in turn, each lit step as bright as its velocity. A melodic row is one line of steps on a note you choose beside it. A drum row opens out into a line of steps for each of its sounds, and Sounds folds it shut. Click a step to set it, drag to paint a run, and right-drag to erase one; each drag is one undo step, and Enter or Space sets the step that has the focus. A step you switch on plays its sound while the transport is stopped.

    A melody written in the piano roll off the step grid shows in its row as a small piano roll across the steps. Double-click it, or press Enter on it, to open it in the editor.

    The graph

    Click a row's name to open its graph under it: the values each step carries — Velocity, Pan, Fine pitch, Chance and Velocity range. Draw across the columns or type a value; each sweep is one undo step.

    Each step's pan and fine pitch are heard on the Drum Machine, the One-Shot Sampler, the Multisampler and the Percussion Sampler, as the song plays and in exports. The Modelled Synth, which plays the library's instruments, the Section Player and the Layer Rack do not play them yet, and a plugin hears every step centred and in tune.

    Length, step and swing

    Each pattern has its own length of up to 16 Bars, its own Step from 1/4 to 1/32, and its own Swing. Changing the length or the step changes every part in the pattern with it, and a choice that would need more than 256 steps in a row is greyed out.

    The steps past the pattern's end are drawn dimmed. Press or drag across them, or drag the grip on the last step, to make the pattern that long or shorter; or set the count with −, the number and + beside Steps.

    Pattern and Song

    Beside the transport is a switch: Pattern or Song. In Pattern, Play loops the open pattern on its tracks' own instruments, effects and mixer settings, at the song's tempo, without touching the arrangement. A step you switch on while it plays is heard on the next pass, and the rack shows the step that is playing. Open another pattern and that one loops instead. Song puts the playhead back where it was in the song.

    Pattern mode is a way of listening, not an edit: undo, the room and export never see it. Tracks that are not in the pattern are silent, the song's automation does not play, and you can record again once you switch back to Song.

    Automation

    Every fader, pan and device control can be automated. Open a track's automation lane, choose the target, and draw. Points snap to the grid; hold Alt to place one freely. A curve between two points can be bent by dragging its middle.

    Kata are automation shapes you draw once and stamp anywhere — a swell, a filter sweep, a sidechain-shaped duck — so a gesture you liked is reusable rather than redrawn.

    Recording and comping

    Arm a track, press record, play. Audio is written to disk as it arrives rather than held in memory, so a crash costs you the last block and not the take.

    Record over the same bars again and you get a second take stacked under the first. Choose the parts you want from each and Oscillate assembles the comp. The takes you did not choose are never deleted — that is what Shadow Takes means, and it is why changing your mind a week later costs nothing.

    A count-in is set in Settings ▸ Recording. It is off by default.

    Playing live

    Plug in a MIDI keyboard and choose it under MIDI input in Settings ▸ MIDI. It plays the armed track, as the piano roll's on-screen keyboard does, and if it is unplugged it reconnects by itself when it comes back. Note activity lights as notes arrive, and with no track armed the section tells you so.

    How it plays

    Transpose moves every key on the hardware keyboard, up to four octaves either way. Pitch bend range is how far the pitch wheel bends: two semitones each way unless you change it, up to four octaves; a plugin bends by its own range. Velocity curve is Linear, Soft, where a light touch plays louder, or Hard, where it takes a firm strike to play loud.

    Chords turns each key into a chord in the project's scale: a triad, a seventh or a ninth, as Chord size says. It applies to every key that reaches the armed track — the hardware keyboard, the on-screen one, and the computer keys you type into it.

    The wheels

    The pitch wheel bends the notes you are holding, and a note struck while the wheel is off its rest starts bent. The mod wheel and channel pressure bring in vibrato on the library's instruments. Hosted VST3 and CLAP plugins receive all three, as they would in any other DAW.

    On the stage, and on the pads

    While the stage follows the armed track, what you play on a MIDI keyboard or type into the piano roll's keyboard shows on its instrument; see The Stage. A pad controller can play the Drum Machine's pads once it has learned them; see The drum grid.

    The mixer

    Ctrl+4. A strip per track with a fader, a pan, mute, solo, the device rack and a meter that is drawn from the audio the engine actually produced — never from a prediction.

    The strip's knobs

    Every track's strip has Low, Mid, High, Room, Echo, Heat, Pitch Mod and Granular, and under them a compressor of its own: its switch, a light that shows when it is taking something off, and Threshold, Ratio, Attack, Release and Makeup. The compressor is off until you switch it on, and Granular at 0 % is out of the path. Every knob is saved with the project, can be automated and moves in one undo step. The master's one knob is Granular.

    The knobs are folded away until you press Knobs; folded, the mixer is faders and meters, and the effects keep working.

    The Masking Radar

    Press Radar and Oscillate watches for pairs of tracks fighting for the same band — the bass and the kick, the vocal and the pad — and names the pair and the frequency. It costs CPU while it is on and the tooltip says so.

    A pair that has not been heard yet reads Not heard yet, not "clean". If the session has more tracks than the radar watches, it tells you that too rather than silently showing a subset.

    Loudness and true peak

    Press Loudness for integrated LUFS, short-term, the range and true peak, plus a table of what each streaming service targets and what it would do to your master. Each target carries the date it was last checked.

    If the meter ever fell behind under load it says so — "the meter fell behind, so some audio was never measured". A number with no warning beside it means a complete measurement.

    The Audio ↔ MIDI balance

    The slider in the Sheets tray blends the project's audio tracks against its MIDI tracks: all the way left plays only the audio, all the way right only the MIDI, and the middle both at full. It is heard at once and glides without clicks, it is saved with the project, and it never moves a fader. Exports, stems and freezes ignore it. Double-click it to put it back in the middle.

    Each strip has the track's padlock too; see Locking a track.

    The Stage

    Ctrl+6, or View ▸ Stage, opens the stage: a 3D instrument and the hands that play a MIDI track on it, in time with the transport — through tempo changes, round the loop and wherever you scrub. It loads the first time you open it, so Oscillate starts no slower for having it.

    Follow chooses the track. Left on The selected track, the stage plays whichever MIDI track you select in the arrangement or the mixer. While the stage is open, resting the pointer on the ruler shows a small picture of the stage at that moment.

    What stands

    Pianos, guitars, basses and drums are each played by hands of their own. The factory Grand, Acoustic, Upright and Honky-Tonk Pianos, the Guitar, Nylon, Electric and Muted Guitars, and the Pluck, Slap and Upright Basses each stand as themselves, and an Electric Guitar into the Guitar Amp stands as a rock guitar. Any other instrument plays on the grand piano.

    A Drum Machine track stands as a 16-pad controller played by finger-drumming hands, its pads lighting as they are hit. Any other drum track stands as a studio kit played with sticks and feet.

    Instrument stands another piano, guitar or bass for a track, or the pad controller or the drum kit for a drum track. It changes the look and the hands only: the sound is still the track's own instrument.

    Each instrument's controls

    • A piano: Key labels on all keys, the white keys or none, and Finger numbers, 1 to 5.
    • A guitar: what it Plays — the chords, the melody or a counter-melody — with strumming and picking hands, slides, hammer-ons and pull-offs.
    • A bass: four or five Strings on the Pluck and Slap Basses. On four, notes below the low E are played an octave up.
    • A guitar or a bass: Backing — off, the track is soloed and heard alone; on, the other tracks play along — a Left-handed player, Finger numbers on the fretting hand and, on the electric and rock guitars and the Pluck Bass, the Fret wire's profile and metal, which change how it looks and not how it sounds.
    • The drum kit: a Left-handed drummer.

    Cameras

    View looks from the Front, the Top or the Side, or closes in on a hand: the left or right hand on a piano, the fretting or picking hand on a guitar, the hands on the pads, the hands or the feet at the kit. Click the stage to give it the focus, and V glides to the next view and Shift+V back. Everywhere else those keys keep their usual jobs.

    While the stage shows, Alt+V and Alt+Shift+V step the view the same way from anywhere, wherever the focus is.

    Quality and skin tone

    Quality is Auto, or a fixed High, Medium or Low. Auto judges a quality by its slowest frames rather than its average, so it lowers the detail only where the machine really stutters, and says so when it does. Skin tone gives the hands one of six tones. Both are kept on this computer.

    Playing the stage

    When the stage follows the armed track you can play it: click the piano's keys, a guitar's strings or the pads, and what you play on a MIDI keyboard or type into the piano roll's keyboard shows on the instrument. While a MIDI keyboard's pitch wheel, mod wheel or pressure is off its rest, a small readout shows it. Following any other track, the header tells you to arm it.

    The music desk

    The sheets on the stage's music desk are the ones the Sheets panel engraves for the track the stage follows; see Sheets. Their pages turn by themselves at the pace set in Settings ▸ Appearance — twice as fast at Quick, a cut at Reduce — and the note the hands are playing is lit.

    For everyone

    Every control on the stage can be reached from the keyboard and is named for a screen reader, and the stage itself is named for what stands and the track it follows — Stage: Grand Piano, following Keys. With reduced motion on, in Settings ▸ Appearance or by your system's setting, the camera cuts from one view to the next instead of gliding, and the hands keep playing.

    On macOS and Linux

    On macOS the stage plays along with the song, as it does on Windows: a Mac draws 3D on a real graphics card. Linux draws Oscillate's window with WebKit, which may be drawing 3D on the processor without saying so, so there the stage stands still to keep the app smooth, and says why: it draws a frame for each change — when you stop, move the playhead or change the view — rather than playing along, and the camera cuts instead of gliding. A Mac with no graphics card at all, such as a virtual machine, stands still the same way.

    When it cannot

    A window that cannot draw WebGL 2 shows 3D view unavailable and why, never a blank stage. A track with more notes than the stage plays is named, with how many it has and how many the stage can take: split it into shorter tracks, or follow another.

    Sheets

    Ctrl+7, View ▸ Sheets, or the Sheets key in the titlebar shows the Sheets panel: any MIDI track as sheet music, following the playhead. It docks under the stage, over the editor, and is hidden until you open it. It always stays in the main window, because its pages turn on the main window's clock. Nothing of it loads until a sheet is first shown, so Oscillate starts no slower for having it.

    What a sheet holds

    Each MIDI track that holds notes is engraved on a treble, bass or grand staff, chosen from the notes it plays. The time signature and the key change where the project's do, each section marker gets a rehearsal mark, silent bars become multi-bar rests, and the pages look like paper, with the project's name and Page n of N. The staves are spaced by what they hold, so drum beams, a guitar's low notes over its TAB and the words never collide. Two hundred bars engrave in about a quarter of a second.

    A sheet is read only. It is a view of its track: edit the notes in the piano roll and the sheet follows.

    TAB and drum sheets

    A guitar's track is set in treble clef an octave down (8vb) over six-line TAB, with chord diagrams and strum arrows. A bass is set over four-line TAB, or five lines on a 5-string. The frets are the ones the stage's hands play. A drum track writes which hand or foot plays each hit under it: R, L, RF or LF.

    Lyrics under the notes

    The words in a track's Lyric Lane are set under its notes, and a syllable held with _ draws its extender line; see The Lyric Lane.

    The tray

    The band along the top of the panel. The full score's chip comes first, then a chip for each MIDI track that holds notes, in the arrangement's order; when they outrun the band it scrolls sideways. Each track's chip carries:

    • its key number and its name: click the name to read its sheet, and again to go back;
    • M and S: the track's own mute and solo, the same two as on its header and its mixer strip (Ctrl-click S to solo it beside the others);
    • Lock: the track's lock; see Locking a track;
    • on Windows and macOS, a grip that drags the track's MIDI out of Oscillate, onto the desktop or into another program. Click grips to pick several tracks and drag them together. The full score's grip, Drag all, drags every sheet's track.

    After the chips come the Audio ↔ MIDI slider (see The Audio ↔ MIDI balance) and the PDF and Print keys, which are greyed out while a PDF or a print is being made.

    The desk

    Under the tray, the desk lays out every sheet's first page as a card, in the tray's order. Click one, or press Enter on it, to read it. The cards are drawn while the song is stopped; open the desk mid-song and it shows the sheets' names until the song stops.

    The reader

    A sheet you open lies full width in the panel, its pages one under another. While the Sheets panel shows, 1 to 9 open the first nine sheets in the tray's order, 1 being the full score; the same key again, or Esc, goes back to the desk. A text field you are typing in keeps its digits. Every one of these keys can be changed in Settings ▸ Shortcuts.

    As the song plays, the pages turn by themselves, keeping the line being played in view with the next one under it. The notes lit are always the ones sounding: a muted track lights nothing, while a track is soloed only the soloed tracks light, and on the full score each note lights on its own track's staff. The section list moves the playhead to a section, and every sheet turns with it. Page n of N says where you are.

    When it cannot

    With no MIDI track holding a note, the panel says so. A track with more notes than a sheet can set keeps its chip, and its sheet names how many it plays and how many a sheet holds: split it into shorter tracks to read it as a sheet.

    Lessons

    Ctrl+8, View ▸ Lessons, or the Lessons key in the titlebar shows the Lessons panel: fifteen courses, learned a day at a time. It opens as a column beside the arrangement and always stays in the main window. Nothing of it loads until you first open it, so Oscillate starts no slower for having it.

    The lessons' own text, each step's words, is in English for now; every key, label and message around it is in your language.

    The courses

    The Courses tab lists the fifteen: Piano, Reading, Rhythm, Ear training, Theory for producers, Songwriting, Lyric Writing, Rap & Flow, Song Arrangement, Mixing, Finger Drumming, Drum Kit, Guitar, Classical Guitar and Bass. Open one to see its map of sections, weeks and Days, how many Days you have passed, and the clocks: this lesson, all lessons and the days practised. Continue opens the Day and the step where you stopped. A Day you have not reached yet still opens, and says so.

    Today

    Today plays the Day you opened, step by step: Watch, Try, In time, Perform, an ear question to answer (Listen), a song to Make, and the rest. A step that plays puts its music on real tracks on the course's instrument, with a bar's count-in, and Oscillate's own transport plays it; the stage lights the keys, the neck or the pads the step asks for. Press Start and play along on a MIDI keyboard, or on your instrument if the course listens. A step passes at three in a row.

    Beside it are the practice tools: one hand while the stage plays the other, a loop of the bars you choose, and the tempo, which climbs towards the step's own as you get it right. A Day you have mastered comes back later for review.

    After a take the grade says what went well and what to fix, naming the bar, the beat and the fix, at most three things, the costliest first. A take heard through your input says it was judged by ear. In a making step, a target you missed on purpose can be marked with Mark intended, and it stops counting.

    Show me

    Show me plays the step for you first, the hands up close on the stage, at half, three-quarter or full speed, and can loop a stretch of it. Nothing is listened to and nothing is graded. Back to the step returns to it.

    Read Aloud

    Switched on, Read Aloud reads the step's text in one of your computer's own voices, lighting each word as it is read and keeping its line in view. No voice is shipped with Oscillate: with none installed it says so, and one can be added in your system's settings. Its bar has Rewind, Read and Pause, Stop, Fast-forward, the pace from half speed to double, the Voice and a reading clock. While the focus is in the Lessons panel, Space reads or pauses, and Shift+← and Shift+→ go back or on a sentence.

    Listening to you

    In a course that can hear you, the lesson bar's Listen switch has the lesson hear your instrument or your voice in place of the MIDI keys. It hears the input of the audio device chosen in Settings ▸ Audio: notes and their timing, chords, the strokes of a drum kit and the syllables of a rap. Input… chooses which of its channels, or every channel mixed, and shows the level and the chord it hears.

    Tuner shows how many cents sharp or flat a note is, chromatic or string by string for a guitar or a bass, with A4 anywhere from 415 to 466 Hz. Calibrate measures how long a sound takes to leave Oscillate and come back in, so what you play lands on time: Loop back sends eight clicks through a cable from the output to the input, or your speakers into the microphone; Play along has you clap or play on each click. Keep keeps the measurement for that input. Until an input is calibrated, Oscillate uses its own estimate and labels it as one. While a lesson listens, the tuner and the calibration wait.

    The Listening light is on exactly while the input is open, and only then is anything heard. What is heard is analysed on this computer as it arrives and let go: nothing is recorded, kept or sent. See Privacy and the network.

    Monitor, in the lesson bar, plays your input through the lesson's own track so you can hear yourself, a guitar or a bass through the amp. It is off by default, because a microphone feeds back through speakers: wear headphones. It is remembered for each course, and it records nothing either.

    Making steps

    A making step opens Oscillate's own tools in the lesson's project: the arrangement with the step's starting song and its sections as markers, the Lyric Lane and its flow grid, or the mixer with the step's loop. Check measures what you made against the step's targets, a mixing target on the mix itself, rendered and measured, and says what it found. Load the example brings in an example, keeping your song as it is now as a version first. In Rap & Flow, Rap it hears a take through your input and times each syllable against where it is placed; the take is never recorded. Each making step keeps a notebook page.

    Your project, set aside

    A lesson works in a project of its own. Yours is set aside in memory, untouched, with its unsaved changes and its undo history, and a band along the top of the panel says so while a lesson is open. Back to my project puts it back exactly as it was, and so do New, Open, a room and quitting. A lesson cannot open while a room is shared.

    History and the portfolio

    History shows your progress in each course and the clocks; every notebook page, each opening its Day at its step; your song's named versions, each compared with the song now and brought back in one undo step; and the lead sheet, which opens in the Sheets panel. Portfolio PDF makes a PDF of your song. All of it is kept in Oscillate's data folder on this computer.

    If Freally Handscore has been used on this computer, its lessons' progress, its portfolio and the words you told it to keep as typed are brought over the first time the lessons need them, once, and Handscore's own files are never changed.

    The classics

    Classics holds 62 public-domain songs, searchable by title, and the passages: a scale, an arpeggio, a hand crossing over and more. Open one as a lesson, into the lesson's own project, or as a project, added after what is already in yours in one undo step.

    Only a classic's chords may be dragged out of Oscillate. Every track a classic adds for its melody stays marked as the classic's, through a rename, an undo and a save, and a drag that would take one out, from its track, the arrangement, the Sheets tray or Drag all, is refused and says why.

    Plugins

    Oscillate hosts VST3 and CLAP, and each plugin runs in a process of its own. A plugin that crashes greys out that device and leaves your session running.

    Your plugins appear in the File Explorer under Plugins. Drag one onto a track, or select the track and click it. Open its own editor from the device rack — the real interface you bought, not a list of sliders — and closing and reopening it brings it back at the size it was.

    Delay compensation is graph-wide and is proved by a null test: the same signal through a latency-reporting device and around it, cancelling to silence. A plugin this machine does not have becomes a named placeholder that still costs its samples, so a project opened somewhere else stays in time.

    The stock devices

    Seventy-three instruments and effects ship inside Oscillate, including Freally Secretarium. Every panel is drawn from the device's own description, so the readout on a knob is the number the DSP is using rather than a second one that agrees most of the time.

    Faces

    A device shows what it is doing to the sound passing through it, live, while its card is showing. An equaliser or an amp draws its curve over the spectrum going in and coming out; a compressor, the limiter or a gate draws its level curve with the sound riding it, the reduction scrolling against the threshold, and its needle; the de-esser draws its band over the spectrum; a saturator draws its transfer curve with the sound as a dot on it; a stereo device draws its field and how well the two sides agree; the delays, the reverbs and the modulation effects draw the level over the bar, and a reverb how fast its tail falls. With reduced motion on, the readings update and nothing scrolls.

    Every face is measured from the device's own sound, never drawn from its settings, and drawing them costs the audio nothing you can measure. Up to sixteen show live at once; past that a card is drawn at rest and says so. An export, a bounce or a freeze never draws into a card.

    Recipe strings

    A device's settings copy as one line you can paste into a chat. A whole rack — stock devices and hosted plugins together — saves as a file and recalls onto any track in one undo step, including on a machine that is missing one of its plugins.

    Suggest

    Each of Oscillate's own effect cards has Suggest. It plays the track alone up to that card, offline, for up to 90 seconds from where the part first sounds; works out what the part is from the track's name; and suggests a setting, with its reasons in sentences: an equaliser levels the worst resonance, a compressor takes the reduction that suits the part, the de-esser sits on the sibilance it measured, a delay lands on the tempo's grid. Nothing changes until you press Apply, which sets every control it names in one undo step. A device whose job is taste says so and suggests nothing, and a hosted plugin has no Suggest.

    Suggest for the strip's knobs, at the top of the selected track's rack, does the same for the strip's Low, High and compressor. It is arithmetic on what was measured, not a model, so the same mix gets the same suggestion every time. To set the whole mix at once, see Mix it for me.

    Oscillate Doubler

    Doubles a part as if it were sung or played again: up to four Voices, each detuned by up to 30 cents (Detune), delayed by 5 to 40 ms (Delay) and spread across the stereo field (Spread), blended with Mix. The voices drift from the project's seed and the device's own Seed, so an export prints the same every time and two doublers in one song drift apart. Its presets are Tape Double, Wide Double, Stack and Subtle.

    Oscillate Ducker

    Turns a track down while another track plays, the bass making room for the kick. Choose the other track in the card's Sidechain from: it is heard after that track's devices and before its fader, so a muted kick still keys it, and the two are kept in time whatever delay either track's devices add. Depth, Threshold, Attack, Release, Hold and Sidechain HPF shape it, and its face draws what it takes off. Its presets are Kick Pump, Gentle Duck and Voice Over.

    The key is kept through a rename, a save and a room. If the key track is deleted, the card says so and the sound passes untouched; an undo brings the key back.

    Devices that read the arrangement

    Six of them do something no plugin can, because a plugin cannot see the arrangement it is inside. A compressor already holding the chorus when it arrives; a gate that opens for a hit two bars before it happens; a delay that empties into the bar line so the tail runs out instead of being cut. Reading two bars of audio ahead is latency and everybody pays for it; reading the structure costs nothing, and all of them declare zero.

    The Drum Machine

    Sixteen pads, laid out like a pad controller with pad 1 at the bottom left, playing one of four built-in kits: Sub, Floor, Neon and Wire. Each hit is chosen from three layers by how hard you play and alternates between two takes, so a roll never repeats itself exactly. Projects that used the Pad Grid open on the Drum Machine with their levels and chokes kept.

    Select a pad to shape its sound: its tune, a pitch sweep, an envelope, its pan, a filter and a choke group, and up to four Layers, each of which you can Reverse or Normalise. The layers take turns By velocity or In turn, a round robin. Reset to the kit's sound puts the pad back.

    Beside the pads, One-shots lists the kit's own sounds and the eleven Drum Packs, by kind. Click one to hear it, then put it on the selected pad or add it there as a layer. Add samples… puts your own files on the pads.

    The Guitar Amp and the rock guitars

    Guitar Amp is an amp and a 4×12 cabinet with two voicings, Crunch and High Gain, and Input and Output trims of up to 24 dB either way. It goes on any track.

    The Chains shelf in the File Explorer holds two rock guitars ready to play, Overdriven Guitar and Distortion Guitar: the Electric Guitar into the Guitar Amp, then an echo and a small room, levelled to sit where the dry guitar did. Load puts one on the selected MIDI track in place of its devices, in one undo step.

    Granular Wash

    One knob, Amount, turns a sound into a cloud of grains that keeps the note and adds the octaves above and below it, with an echo that carries it on for seconds. It starts at 50 %; at 100 % you hear the wash alone.

    Envelopes on every instrument

    Every instrument in the library has four envelopes you can switch on: Volume, Filter, Pitch and Pan. Each starts from a shape — Init, Pluck, Pad, Swell, Gate or Bounce — or you draw your own on its curve: drag a point, or double-click to add a point or take one away, up to 16 of your own.

    An envelope can hold at a point while the key is down and loop back from it, lock to the tempo, follow velocity and the key, restart each bar, and morph between its A and B shapes. A drawn shape is saved in the project and in rack files, but not in a pasted settings line. With every envelope off, an instrument sounds exactly as it did.

    The File Explorer

    Ctrl+1. A home screen of places — Instruments, Audio Effects, MIDI Effects, Plugins — that you click into, a Choose folder button, and the folders you have added listed by name.

    The filter searches what a device is as well as what it is called, so typing valve finds Tube Saturator and Amp Head.

    The preview

    Click a device and you hear its own demonstration — a note chosen and a length measured per instrument — with a waveform, a playhead, a loop, and ←/→ to run it backwards or forwards. Point it at a folder of your own and it previews your samples the same way. Four of the instruments are samplers and say so rather than playing silence: they cannot sound until you give them a file with Add samples… on the device.

    The instruments

    All 1,648 instruments are on the Instruments and Factory Expansion shelves, in folders by family, and the list scrolls smoothly however long it is. Click a row, or move through the list with the arrow keys, to hear it; resting the pointer on a row plays it too. With a MIDI track selected, what you hear is that track's own notes from the playhead, played through the instrument you are on. Add puts the instrument on the selected track.

    The star on a row puts the instrument in Starred. In My Sounds, Save sound keeps the instrument on the selected MIDI track, with its settings as they are now, under a name and a folder of your own, ready to Add to any track.

    PACK

    Every instrument row has a PACK key, which adds a one-shot of it to the One-shot pack under the browser, up to 25. Play each one, swap it for another take of the same instrument, or take it out. Export writes every one-shot in the pack as a WAV file into a folder you choose, and never writes over a file that is already there.

    Dragging a file in

    Drag a .wav or a .mid straight from Windows Explorer or Finder onto a bar in the arrangement and it lands where you dropped it. MIDI goes onto MIDI tracks, audio onto audio tracks, and the wrong kind is refused with a reason rather than being given a lane of its own.

    A dropped file is taken into the project, not referenced. Drop something out of a temporary folder and delete the original, and the clip still plays.

    The room

    Ctrl+5. Press Create a room code, wait for the code, and send it to whoever you are working with. They paste it, send you theirs back, you paste that into Let them in, and you are in the same session.

    What you get

    • A roster, with a colour each, and their cursors moving on your arrangement.
    • Chat. It lasts as long as the room and nothing is written to disk.
    • Voice, camera tiles and screen share — all off until you press something.
    • Your undo is yours. Ctrl+Z takes back your last change and never somebody else's, even while their edits are arriving.
    • Takeover — hand one track to somebody and take it back with one key. While they hold it they are the only writer for it, including you.

    What never travels

    A take you recorded travels. A sample you bought never does. When somebody joins a session that uses a sample pack they do not have, they get a list called Samples you need and the reason: Oscillate never sends samples between computers. Byte-identical is what piracy produces, and this product will not be a route to it.

    When it does not connect

    Roughly one connection in ten will not form, and Oscillate names which of nine causes it was — from no network was found to your network gives every server a different address. That last one needs a relay in the middle; there is a field for your own in Settings ▸ Collaboration.

    Kumite and the Split Sheet

    Kumite is a beat battle mode where each turn owns its own bars. The countdown is a countdown, but the bars are the rule — so two computers can never disagree about whose edit was legal.

    The Split Sheet is who wrote what, derived from the edit log, with work anybody undid netted out. It is a starting point for a conversation about credit, not a legal document, and it says so.

    The Lyric Lane

    Select a vocal track and press Lyrics. A lane of words appears, locked to the playhead. Add lines, move a line a bar earlier or later, split a line into syllables, and point a recorded take at the words it belongs to.

    Import a .txt or .lrc file and the words are copied into the project — move the file afterwards and the words are still there.

    The type is meant to be sung from across a room, not read leaning in. Every lyric edit is an operation, so Ctrl+Z takes it back like everything else.

    Typing with the dictionary

    The line editor completes the word you are typing from a 327,000-word English dictionary: the rest of the likeliest word shows after the caret, and Tab takes it. A strip under the line shows the word as you typed it and its suggestions. Shift+Tab, or the ✓ beside the word, keeps it as typed: a word you keep is never corrected again and is offered from then on. Your kept words stay on this computer.

    The Spelling switch in the lane corrects a misspelled word as it ends. It is on by default when Oscillate is in English and off in the other languages, because the dictionary is English's, and your choice is remembered on this computer. Backspace right after a correction puts your word back. Completion and the tools below work the same with the switch on or off.

    Syllables on their notes

    Type - between a word's syllables, and _ to hold a syllable over the next note. On a track that plays notes, Split puts each syllable on its note, and each line shows how it fits the notes under it: 4/4 when they match, +2 for two syllables too many, −1 for a note left over. A line longer than its notes cannot be split, and says why; a word a rest breaks in two is named.

    Stress, rhymes and flow

    • Stress marks marks the stressed syllable of each word in the lane, and the prompter sets it bold.
    • Rhymes colours each line's last word by its rhyme, puts the line's letter in its section's scheme before it and the section's whole scheme — AABB, ABAB… — on its first line, and flags a stock phrase as common.
    • Flow grid lays each bar's syllables on the sixteenth grid, with the count, a mark where the bar's last syllable lands on beat four, the pocket and the prosody: the share of stressed syllables that land on a strong beat, a long note or a peak of the melody.
    • Rap it, in the flow grid, times a recorded take you select on the track against the placed syllables, and marks each one early or late. It reads a take, never a microphone, and it generates nothing.

    The lead sheet

    The Lead sheet key in the lane opens the track's melody, its words and the chord symbols in the Sheets panel's reader. The chords are the track's own, or those of the first other track that strikes chords.

    Export

    File ▸ Export companion folder… writes stems and MIDI that any other DAW can open. Stems line up at bar 1, so importing them somewhere else does not mean nudging thirty files into place.

    Format, sample rate, bit depth, dither and where it goes are in Settings ▸ Export.

    A song can be any length: the companion folder and the masters are written to disk as they render rather than held in memory. A WAV or AIFF file cannot pass 4 GB, and an export that would is refused and leaves nothing behind. A track dragged out as audio is still limited to ten minutes.

    Session cards. Ctrl+Shift+C, or File ▸ Session card…, renders a 1200×630 picture of the session — tempo, key, track and clip counts, length and the shape of the arrangement — to post. A room code cannot go on one: it is kilobytes long and contains your public address, so the option is present, disabled, and says why.

    The sheets as a PDF

    File ▸ Export sheets as PDF…, or the PDF key in the Sheets tray, makes one A4 PDF: a cover, the credits, every track's sheet and the full score, saved where you choose in your system's own save dialog, which suggests the project's name. A line counts the pages as they are made, then says that the PDF is saved, that there is nothing to put in it, that it would be too large, or why it failed. Cancelling the dialog says nothing.

    The cover gives the key, the tempo and the time signature, each change with the bar it comes at, the number of bars, and the notes in all and track by track. The credits name the writers, whose notes are still in the song, and the producers, whose clips or devices are, from the project's own history: under the names set in Settings ▸ Collaboration ▸ Your name in a room, or a collaborator's from their handle. Someone who never gave a name is counted, never printed as an id. Vocalists, samples and a label are left out, because the history cannot know them.

    Printing the sheets

    File ▸ Print sheets…, or the Print key in the tray, sends the same pages to your system's print dialog: nothing else of the window, on white A4 paper in either theme. In Arabic the cover and the credits read right to left.

    Final check

    Ctrl+9, View ▸ Final check, or its key in the titlebar opens the Final check: a master for each place the song is going, each checked before it leaves. It is a window of its own, like the Mixer, and its ⇲ docks it under the Mixer in the main window. The destinations come first, then three tabs: Speakers, Loudness Staircase and Spec Gate.

    A master for each destination

    Tick where the song is going: Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube, Amazon Music, Tidal, Deezer, SoundCloud, a CD, a club, or broadcast to EBU R 128 or ATSC A/85. Each row gives its loudness target, its true-peak ceiling and the file it wants. A row says whether the figure is the service's own or Oscillate's default where the service publishes none, with where it came from and the day it was read. A target is guidance, never a promise that a service will accept a file. What you tick is remembered on this computer.

    Export the masters makes one master for each ticked row, together, from the same mix: each is limited to its ceiling, brought to its loudness and written in its format, then measured from the file on disk, and the table says whether it meets its row. They are saved together in one folder, named after the project, where Settings ▸ Export sends your exports.

    Hear it as

    The Speakers tab plays what you are hearing as a Phone, a Car, a Laptop, a Small speaker or Earbuds would, or Flat, as it is. Each speaker is level-matched, so a switch changes the tone and not the loudness, and the switch crossfades without a click: go between a speaker and Flat (A) for an A/B. It is monitoring only: what you hear here never reaches an export, a bounce or a master, and every launch starts flat. Each speaker is an approximation of a kind of speaker, never a measurement of any product.

    Every master exported is also checked as a phone's speaker and a car's cabin would play it: how much of the bass a phone still carries, what is lost in mono, how wide the low end is, and whether it builds up at 80–150 Hz or 200–400 Hz in a car, each with what to do about it.

    The Loudness Staircase

    Loudness Staircase measures each section of the song, from one marker to the next, on its own, against a step for its kind (Intro, Verse, Pre-chorus, Chorus, Bridge or Outro) within a tolerance, and checks that each chorus lifts over the verse before it by the margin in Chorus over verse. Name the markers in English for their kind to be known. Measure the mix draws the steps over the song's loudness, with every figure in a table. The targets are kept on this computer, not in the project.

    The Spec Gate

    Spec Gate checks an exported file, one of the masters or any file with Check a file…, against a delivery target: streaming, loud or quiet; broadcast, EBU R 128 or US (ATSC A/85); a CD master; a distributor's WAV; or your own. Its thirteen rules cover the loudness, the loudest 3 seconds and 400 ms, the loudness range, the true and sample peaks, the sample rate, the bit depth, the channels, the length, the silence at each end and clipped samples in a row; each gives what was measured, the margin and the simplest fix. Where one change of gain meets every level rule it says how much, and where none can, it says that instead.

    A target can be edited and stamped with Mark verified today; one not verified for 180 days is shown as unverified. The report saves as HTML, JSON or PDF. A pass means the file met the rules of the target you chose, never that a service will accept it.

    Mix it for me

    Mix it for me, beside Export the masters, sets the whole mix for the first destination ticked: every unlocked, unmuted part's effect cards to their suggestion (see Suggest), each part's level by what it is, and on the master a True-Peak Limiter that lands it on the row's loudness under its ceiling. Locked and muted tracks are left as they are, and so is a fader with volume automation; if the master's Granular is on it is turned off, because it plays after the limiter. The report says what it did.

    It is one undo step: undo puts the whole mix back. It renders each part offline to measure it, so on a large session it takes a while.

    Settings

    Ctrl+,. Every change applies immediately and persists per machine. There is no Apply button, because a settings dialog with one is a settings dialog that can be wrong about what is in effect.

    Audio

    Choose your output and how much of the device to take. On Windows you can ask for Exclusive, which gives you the lowest latency the hardware can do and silences every other application. Oscillate tells you which mode you actually got and why — either the stream opened exclusively or this machine would not open an exclusive stream, so the device is still shared. Never a silent downgrade.

    Appearance and language

    Both themes, eighteen languages, and the choice you make wins over the machine's on the next launch. Right-to-left is real, not a text alignment. Speed and Reduced motion sit beside them: Quick turns the title twice as fast, and Reduce stops every animation, whatever the system says.

    Updates

    Settings ▸ Updates. Oscillate looks for an update only when you press Check for updates, or at launch if you switch that on — and it is off by default.

    The check requests one static file and sends nothing about you, this machine or your projects. When an update is found you are shown the version, the date, the changelog and the size, and you choose. Every artifact is signed; one whose signature does not verify is refused with an error rather than installed.

    With checking at launch switched off, nothing is contacted and the panel shows no result at all — Last checked stays Never. It never draws "you are up to date" over a check that did not happen, because those are different facts and only one of them would be true.

    Privacy and the network

    Oscillate needs no account, collects no telemetry, and never sends your projects, your audio or anything about you or your machine anywhere. Recording, editing, generation, playback, mixing and export are entirely local.

    There are exactly three occasions on which it uses the network:

    1. A collaboration session you opened or joined. Directly to the other people in it, peer to peer, with no server in the middle.
    2. A STUN lookup, only while a room is being established, to discover your public address. The server contacted is shown in Settings and can be replaced with your own — or switched off entirely, which means the room contacts nobody at all.
    3. An update check, only when you press the button or switch on checking at launch. Off by default. See Updates.

    This is enforced by a build gate rather than promised: no HTTP client is reachable from any part of Oscillate except the collaboration and update components named above, and the gate proves it by walking the dependency graph rather than by searching for names. Crash reports are written to your disk, shown to you in full, and leave the machine only if you choose to send one.

    Live listening. When a lesson listens to you, or you use the tuner or calibrate your input, Oscillate hears the input of the audio device chosen in Settings ▸ Audio, and only while the listening light is on. What it hears is analysed on your computer as it arrives, into notes, chords, drum strokes, syllables and timing, and then let go. No recording is made, no audio is written to disk, and nothing is sent anywhere.

    The only thing listening keeps is a small settings file, listen.json, in Oscillate's data folder on this computer: the input you chose, your A4 tuning, which courses listen, each course's Monitor switch, and each input's measured latency in milliseconds. That is numbers and names, never sound. Monitor, off by default, plays your input through the lesson's own track so you can hear yourself; it records nothing either. A build gate fails if listening's code reaches for any file but that one, and a test runs a whole listening session and finds nothing else left behind.

    The limits, stated plainly

    • Four producers in a room, because every peer sends to every other peer and the fifth connection is where an ordinary home upload gives out.
    • Roughly one connection in ten will not form without a relay, and Oscillate does not ship one — a relay costs bandwidth forever, and that is a running bill a free application cannot carry.
    • Samples never cross the wire. A take you recorded does; a sample you bought does not.
    • Six devices are named and greyed with no controls, each for a written reason rather than a shrug.
    • The lessons' text is in English until the beta. Every control around it is in your language.
    • A moved or stretched audio clip is drawn as its file. The waveform shows the recording, not the sound that plays.
    • A sampler with no file cannot be rendered to audio. Converting a pattern on a track whose sampler is empty is refused by name rather than handed back as silence.
    • Screen share is Windows and Linux. macOS has no display-capture route out of an embedded webview that does not need private API in a notarised binary, so the button is absent there and says why.
    • A dropped peer is not detected yet. If somebody's network goes, the roster keeps showing them.

    Troubleshooting

    There is no sound

    Check Settings ▸ Audio first: the device, and which mode you actually got. If another application has the interface in exclusive mode, Oscillate cannot open it and says so. A track that is silent when others are not is usually soloed elsewhere, muted, or has its instrument slot empty.

    A plugin does not appear

    Settings ▸ Plugins lists the folders being scanned and what was found. A plugin that failed to load is listed as blocked with the reason, and can be unblocked to try again. Oscillate hosts VST3 and CLAP; it does not host VST2 or AU.

    The room will not connect

    Read the reason it gives — there are nine of them and they are different problems. Your network gives every server a different address means you need a relay, which goes in Settings ▸ Collaboration. Check that the code you pasted is the whole code: they are long, and a chat client that wrapped one will have broken it.

    Something crashed

    A report is written to your machine automatically. Settings ▸ General names the most recent one and opens the folder. The report contains the version, the operating system, the audio device and the plugin list by name — never your project, never a path from your disk, never a room code. It leaves your computer only if you choose to send it.

    A panel went blank but the music kept playing

    That is a different failure from a crash and it is the one most people do not report. The audio thread runs none of the interface's code, so playback continues and your project is intact. A report was still written; Settings will name it.
