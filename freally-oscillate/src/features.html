What it does

A room with no server behind it

Send a room code to whoever you are working with and up to three other producers can join your session: their cursors on your timeline, chat, voice, camera tiles and screen share. Peer to peer, with no account and no server in the middle.

Your undo is yours

Ctrl+Z takes back your last change and never somebody else's, even while their edits are arriving. The edit log behind it is also the room's sync, Session Rewind and the Split Sheet.

Takeover

Hand one track to a collaborator and take it back with one key. While they hold it they are the only writer for it — including you — and that is enforced where edits are checked, not only in the interface.

Kumite

A beat battle mode in which each turn owns its own bars. The countdown is a countdown; the bars are the rule, so two computers can never disagree about whose edit was legal.

The Split Sheet

Who wrote what, derived from the edit log, with work anybody undid netted out. A starting point for a conversation about credit, not a legal document — and it says so.

Takes travel; samples never do

A take recorded in the room lands on everybody's timeline. A sample you bought is never sent: the room sends the reference, and a collaborator without the pack is told which samples they need.

MixSir — the mix on your phone

Send the mix and every part to a phone on your Wi-Fi from a QR code. It opens in the phone's browser with nothing to install, mixes there with gain, pan, mute and solo, and saves any part to the phone as a file. It travels only over your own network, behind a key made for that pairing alone. New in v0.16.0.

The arrangement

Unlimited audio and MIDI tracks, clips drawn from what they are, automation on every fader, pan and device control, comping, groups, markers, and tempo and time-signature maps. A pattern placed ten times is one pattern: edit it once and every placement changes, until you make one unique.

The clip launcher and several arrangements

Launch clips and whole scenes on the bar, let one scene follow another, and record what you launched back into the arrangement in one undo step. A project holds several arrangements of one song, each with its own clips and markers over the same tracks and mixer. Patterns paint along a lane, a zoom bar sits above the timeline, and every tool has its key. New in v0.16.0.

The Cut and Session Rewind

Draw a blade stroke across the arrangement and it splits every clip it crosses, the angle of the stroke setting the crossfade. Scrub the session's history like a video, and branch from any point in it.

The piano roll

Around ninety scales with highlighting, snapping and folding, ghost notes from any other clip, a velocity lane with a handle per note, lanes for how often each note plays and how far its velocity wanders, and a keyboard where one key can play a whole chord.

The Pattern Rack

Build a part row by row above the arrangement, clicking steps right in the rack: each row on one line with its track's own light, pan, volume and solo, and a graph of every step's velocity, pan, fine pitch and chance. Pattern mode loops the open pattern on its tracks' own sounds without touching the song, and Place pattern puts it in the song, linked. New in v0.9.0, polished in v0.10.0.

The Drum Machine and the drum grid

Sixteen pads on four built-in kits, each hit chosen by how hard you play, with up to four layers a pad, chokes, reverse and normalise. The drum grid is a step sequencer with swing and velocity, a Drum Map plays drums written for another kit on yours, and your own pad controller can learn the pads. New in v0.9.0.

The stage

Hands on a 3D piano, guitar, bass, pad controller or drum kit play any MIDI track in time with the transport. Choose the camera, the quality and the skin tone, and play it yourself on the armed track. New in v0.9.0.

Sheet music, TAB and the PDF

Any MIDI track engraved as sheet music in a panel of its own: treble, bass or grand staff, guitar and bass TAB with chord diagrams, drum sheets that say which hand or foot plays each hit, and the words under the notes. The pages turn with the song and light the notes sounding, and the whole song exports as an A4 PDF with a cover and the credits, or prints. New in v0.10.0.

Lessons

Fifteen courses, learned a day at a time: piano, guitar, bass and the drums, reading and rhythm, the ear and theory, songwriting, lyrics, rap, arrangement and mixing. Show me plays each step on the stage first, Read Aloud reads it in your computer's own voice, and the lesson listens to your instrument through your audio interface, on this computer only and recording nothing, then names the bar, the beat and the fix. Your project waits, untouched, while you learn. New in v0.12.0.

The instrument browser

All 1,648 instruments in folders by family, a star for the ones you come back to, sounds of your own, and an audition that plays the selected track's own notes through each instrument. PACK gathers up to 25 one-shots and exports them as WAV files. New in v0.9.0.

Find Similar and Kick and Bass Match

Find Similar ranks the look-alikes of a sound or a MIDI loop across your folders, each with its reason: a re-save, a level change, a trim, a pitch or a stretch, the same rhythm. Kick and Bass Match ranks every bass in a folder against a kick, with the retune that puts the two in tune. Measurement, not a model. New in v0.16.0.

Playing live

A MIDI keyboard plays the armed track with transpose, a bend range, a velocity curve and chords in the project's scale. The pitch wheel, the mod wheel and pressure reach the library's instruments and your plugins. New in v0.9.0.

Recording and comping

Audio recording and a hardware MIDI keyboard on Windows, macOS and Linux. Takes stack under each other, you choose the parts you want, and the takes you did not choose are never deleted. Audio is written to disk as it arrives, so a crash costs the last block rather than the take.

Oscillate Retro Record

The last minutes of your audio input and your MIDI keyboard, kept in this computer's memory, so the take you played before you pressed record can still be saved: as a clip on a track at the bar you played it, or as a WAV with its date and time. It asks first, shows a light while it holds anything, and writes nothing until you save. New in v0.16.0.

Pitch moves and stretch

Move a span of a recording up or down by up to an octave with its formants kept, so a voice stays itself, and fit a clip to the tempo without changing its pitch. Your file is never touched, and every export and every collaborator hears the same. New in v0.12.0.

Your plugins, each in a process of its own

VST3 and CLAP. A plugin that crashes greys out its device and leaves the session running. Delay compensation is graph-wide and proved by a null test, and a plugin this machine does not have becomes a named placeholder, so a project opened somewhere else stays in time.

Its own instruments and effects

Eighty-five devices, among them Freally Secretarium and its 1,648 instruments, a doubler that prints the same every time, a ducker keyed by another track, a kick carve, a reese whose sub stays mono, forty hand-written feels, a siren, a laser-zap delay, a granular rock column and a storm, and, new in v0.17.0, the low-end pair: Oscillate Sub, a sub-bass that follows what you play, and Oscillate Width, which widens above it and never thins the mono sum, the two sharing one crossover when linked. Each panel is drawn from the device's own description, so the number on a knob is the number the audio is using; each face draws the sound passing through it, live; and a device's settings copy as one line you can paste into a chat.

Devices that read the arrangement

Six devices do what a plugin cannot, because a plugin cannot see the song it is in: a compressor already holding the chorus when it arrives, a gate that opens for a hit before it happens, a delay that empties into the bar line. None of the six adds latency.

The mixer

A strip per track with a fader, pan, mute, solo, the device rack, a meter drawn from the audio the engine actually produced, and knobs for Low, Mid, High, Room, Echo, Heat, Pitch Mod and Granular beside a compressor of its own. The Masking Radar names two tracks fighting for the same band and the frequency; the loudness panel shows integrated and short-term LUFS, the range and true peak against what each streaming service targets.

The final check

A master for each destination, from Spotify and Apple Music to a CD, a club and broadcast, exported together from one mix and each measured from its file against its target. Hear the mix as a phone, a car, a laptop, a small speaker or earbuds would play it, level-matched and never printed; the Loudness Staircase checks each section's loudness, and the Spec Gate checks a file against a delivery spec and writes the report. New in v0.12.0.

Suggest, and Mix it for me

Every effect card measures what reaches it and suggests a setting with its reasons, applied in one undo step. Mix it for me sets every part and lands the master on the destination you ticked, in one undo step too. It is arithmetic on the measurement, not a model, so the same mix gets the same answer. New in v0.12.0.

The Lyric Lane

Lyrics as a lane on the timeline, locked to the playhead, imported from .txt or .lrc and copied into the project, in type meant to be sung from across the room. A 327,000-word dictionary completes and corrects as you type, each syllable lands on its note, and stress marks, rhymes with their scheme and a flow grid help you write. New in v0.10.0.

Session cards and Session Timelapse

One shortcut renders a 1200×630 picture of the session to post. Session Timelapse rebuilds the whole session from its edit log as an animated picture with chapters — even a session nobody thought to record.

Your session opens without Oscillate

The companion folder writes stems and MIDI that any other DAW can open, lined up at bar 1, in the format, sample rate and bit depth you choose.

The family look

Knobs, faders and buttons rendered in Blender, light and dark on Freally green, short interface sounds you can switch off, and eighteen languages with real right-to-left — the same look in every Freally app.

Nothing phones home

No account and no telemetry. It uses the network on four occasions only: a room you opened or joined, the address lookup while that room is set up, an update check that is off until you switch it on, and MixSir, which serves your mix to a phone on your own network only while you are sending it. Crash reports stay on your disk unless you choose to send one.

No AI, enforced by the build

No model, no inference, nothing generative. A build gate walks the dependency graph and fails on any inference runtime or model file, so it is a property of the build rather than a sentence on a website.

Why it is built the way it is

No server, so nothing to switch off

A room code is the connection offer itself. With no server there is nothing to keep running, nothing to pay for and nothing anybody can turn off — which is also why a room holds four producers and not forty: every peer sends to every other peer.

One log, four uses

Every edit is an operation in one log. Undo, the room's sync, Session Rewind and the Split Sheet are four readings of it, which is why your undo can be yours alone and why credit can be derived rather than typed in.

A crash is one device, not the session

Every hosted plugin runs in a process of its own, and the application itself cannot even load one. A plugin that falls over greys out its device, and the transport keeps playing.

The number you see is the number in the audio

Meters are drawn from what the engine produced, a knob's readout from the value the DSP is using, and a measurement that fell behind says so. Nothing on screen is a prediction.

It stays on your machine

Your projects, audio and MIDI leave your computer only when you are in a room with somebody — and then they go straight to the people in it, not through anybody's cloud.

What is still coming

Stated plainly, because a feature list that hides its gaps is not worth reading. Everything above is what version 1.0 is planned to have, and this is what still stands between here and there.

What 1.0 will not do

  • Host AU or VST2 plugins. It hosts VST3 and CLAP.
  • Use ASIO. On Windows the low-latency path is WASAPI exclusive, and Oscillate tells you which mode you actually got.
  • Hold more than four producers in a room. The fifth connection is where an ordinary home upload gives out.
  • Relay a connection that will not form. Roughly one in ten will not without a relay in the middle; Oscillate names the cause, and Settings takes your own relay.
  • Share a screen from macOS. Screen share is Windows and Linux; on macOS the button is absent and says why.
