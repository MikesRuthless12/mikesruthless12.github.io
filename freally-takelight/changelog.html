v0.6.12026-10-28Wave 1, Whole

The rest of the first wave: four new modules and two new modes, seven more chains that needed them, and twelve more artists.

Added

  • Resonance Suppressor — finds where your voice rings out over the frequencies around it and turns down only those, only while they ring, across a range you set. It never looks ahead, so it adds no latency and works while you record.
  • Transient Shaper — lifts or softens the start of each sound with Attack, and its ring with Sustain, whatever its level; a held note passes untouched. Its card draws a hit before and after.
  • Spectral Ducker — on a return, a delay or a reverb steps out of your vocal's way only at the frequencies your vocal fills, and comes back between phrases.
  • Pump — dips your vocal on every beat, or every bar, half, eighth or sixteenth note, and brings it back along a curve, in time with your project. Its card draws the dips across a bar.
  • Shimmer — a sixth Reverb character: the hall's tail goes round again an octave up, as much as its Shimmer knob lets.
  • Upward compression in the Multiband — each band lifts what falls under its upward threshold, up to a most you set; at 1:1 it lifts nothing, so chains from before sound as they did.
  • Seven more chains — Olivia Rodrigo, Taylor Swift, Dua Lipa, Young Thug, BTS, Bebe Rexha and Panic! at the Disco, each in the era of a record.
  • Twelve more artists — Lady Gaga in two eras, Lewis Capaldi, Sara Bareilles, The 1975, NF, Mike Posner, Lil Nas X, Robin Thicke, Olivia Dean, Muse, Tinchy Stryder and Tones And I.

v0.6.02026-10-28Roster Wave 1

Forty more artists, a lead and an ad-lib chain for every genre on the roster, and the five modules the new chains needed, each with a card that draws what it does. The docs site lists every chain on its roster page.

Added

  • Forty more artists — each in the era of a record or a decade, from Adele to Twice, and Cardi B and DaBaby with an ad-lib chain too. Search a name, as before.
  • A chain for every genre — each of the 73 genres on the roster, from afrobeats and amapiano to UK garage and West Coast club, has a lead chain and an ad-lib chain of its own; trap has both for the 2000s and the 2010s. An artist on the roster with no chain of their own yet is offered their genre's.
  • Dynamic EQ — two bands, each a bell or a shelf, that cut or lift only while your vocal crosses their threshold, as far as their range allows. Its card draws the whole range and where each band sits now.
  • Multiband — a compressor in three bands, low, mid and high, split where you set, each band with its own threshold, ratio and gain. With nothing taken, the three add back up to your vocal as it was.
  • Flanger and Phaser — the Flanger sweeps a short delay against your vocal, and the Phaser sweeps 4, 8 or 12 stages around a frequency you set; each has its rate, depth, feedback and mix.
  • Auto-pan — moves your vocal between left and right, as a sine or a triangle, at the rate and depth you set; the side it leaves fades, and neither side is ever lifted.
  • Clip — a fifth character for Saturation: it flattens the peaks hard, with a corner just soft enough to stay clean.
  • The roster page — the docs site lists every artist and genre with a chain, era by era, with the chains each has: lead, ad-lib, FX.

Changed

  • Four delays land on the eighth note — the ad-lib delays of Drake's modern era, classic R&B and 2010s trap, and the classic R&B lead's, now repeat on the eighth note as they were meant to, not the dotted eighth.

v0.5.62026-10-27Visual Cards

Every module's card now shows what it does, not only its knobs: the EQs draw their curve over your vocal as it plays, the compressors show where your vocal sits and how much they take, and the rest draw their repeats, their decay, their stereo field and their curve. Each picture is worked out from the same maths that makes the sound.

Added

  • The EQ cards — the Parametric, Console and Program EQ draw their curve over a live spectrum of your vocal, before the EQ (dim) and after it (bright), with the peaks held a moment. Every band has a dot on the curve: drag it to move its frequency and gain together, turn the mouse wheel over it for its width, double-click it to put it back, or use the arrow keys. The Program EQ's dots move between its frequencies, as its knobs do, and the Console EQ's Class A dots sit on the steps it takes. The De-esser draws the band it listens to and the cut it makes, and Trim its high-pass.
  • The compressor cards — the Opto, FET, VCA, Vari-mu, Punch, Vox Leveler, Level Rider and Limiter draw their curve, level in against level out, with a dot where your vocal sits on it now and, beneath, the gain reduction of the last four seconds. Drag the threshold along the curve, or use the arrow keys; it turns the knob that sets it.
  • The Gate's card — its curve, and beneath it the level it hears against its threshold over the last four seconds, shaded where it is shut.
  • The space cards — the Delay's repeats on a timeline against your project's beat and bar, each as loud as the feedback and filters leave it (Ping-pong's left above the line, its right below); the Reverb's decay — the pre-delay, the early reflections and the tail; the Reverse Gate's swell; and the stereo field of the Doubler, the Chorus and Width, seen from above.
  • The colour cards — Saturation, the Exciter and Lo-fi draw their curve and, beside it, the harmonics it adds to your vocal as it is now.

Changed

  • A drag over two knobs is one step to undo — moving an EQ band's dot changes its frequency and gain together, and one Undo takes the whole drag back, however long you held it still.

v0.5.52026-10-24Input Check, Ad-libs & Presets

Set your level before you record, keep your own versions of any chain, and take back any change with one key. Tune's Auto Key can now find the key from the beat.

Added

  • Input Check — press Input Check in the rack and sing or play the take for ten seconds; the take light glows amber while it listens. Then it shows what it heard (peak, level, loudness, noise floor, rumble, sibilance and plosives) and what it suggests: the Input knob, and the gate, high-pass and de-esser the chain already has. Nothing changes until you press Apply, and a module the chain doesn't have says so — nothing is added for you. If the take clips, it tells you to lower your interface's gain.
  • Your presets — Save preset… keeps the chain as you've shaped it, under a name you choose, as a file in Documents › Freally Takelight › Presets. My presets… loads, renames, exports and deletes them, and imports a preset someone sent you (a file that isn't one is refused, with the reason). Your presets appear in the search too, under My presets, with the chain each was made from. When that chain is updated, your preset follows it and keeps your edits.
  • Undo and redo — every change to the sound, up to 100 steps: a knob (one step per drag), a module added or removed, its character, bypass and solo, the sends and faders, the four big knobs, the Input knob, a chain or preset loaded, and Input Check's Apply. The arrows in the top bar, or Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z (Ctrl+Y too; ⌘Z and ⌘⇧Z on a Mac) while the window has the focus. The view, the theme, the language and the A/B reference are never undone, and neither is your DAW's automation; switching between A and B starts that side's history fresh.
  • The Input knob — beside the In meters, the level coming in, from −24 to +24 dB. Input Check sets it, and your DAW can automate it.
  • Auto Key from the beat — Tune can listen to a sidechain input: send your beat to Freally Takelight's sidechain and choose Listens to: Sidechain on Tune's card, and Auto Key follows the beat's chords instead of your voice. Each project remembers its choice; Settings sets it for new ones. When nothing plays on the sidechain, Tune listens to your vocal and says so.

Changed

  • The Open and Save dialogs belong to the window — they open in front of Freally Takelight and keep it behind them until you've chosen.

v0.5.02026-10-24First Light — Drake & the Browser

The first complete version: search an artist, pick an era, press play, and the whole vocal chain loads in one click. Drake's eras and eighteen genre chains arrive, with four big knobs to shape them, a reference to compare against, and a second version of your chain to compare with.

Added

  • The chain browser — type an artist or a genre in the search (Ctrl+K, or Cmd+K on a Mac), and the chains are laid out by era and role as you type, nicknames included. With the field empty it offers the chains you loaded last; for an artist not in the library yet it suggests a genre chain. Every step works from the keyboard.
  • Drake — three eras: So Far Gone era (2009), Take Care era (2011) and Modern era (2016–2026), a lead for each and an ad-lib for the modern era, and an FX chain from the Take Care era that lives on the returns alone.
  • Eighteen genre chains — a lead and an ad-lib for trap (2000s and 2010s), Chicago drill, emo rap, classic R&B, modern pop, rock, country pop and reggaeton.
  • Simple mode — the take light, the chain's name, four big knobs and the In/Out meters; Advanced shows the whole rack. Each project remembers which it was left in, and Settings chooses where a new one opens.
  • Four knobs — Tune (how fast and how naturally the vocal is tuned), Tone (darker or brighter, the de-esser following), Space (every effect send together) and Amount (the whole chain against your dry vocal). With Tune, Tone and Space at the centre and Amount at full, the chain plays as it was made, and every chain loads that way. Your DAW can automate all four.
  • A/B reference — open a finished song or vocal (WAV, AIFF, FLAC or MP3, up to 20 minutes), or drop one on the window, and B plays it from where your DAW is (from the start in the standalone), crossfaded in 20 ms and matched to your vocal's loudness, so you judge the tone and not the level. It is held in memory only: never saved with the project, a preset or anywhere else, and a file on a network drive is refused.
  • Chain compare — two versions of your chain, A and B: B starts as a copy of A, each keeps its own edits, and one click switches between them.
  • Projects remember — the chain, the view, Simple's knobs and both sides of the compare are saved with your project. A project that cannot be read opens on the default chain and says so once.
  • The Freally cursor — a ring with a dot at its heart, which grows over anything you can press.

Changed

  • Toggles — every toggle is gray when off, white under the pointer, and Freally green when on, under the pointer or not.
  • A full rack says so — a rack holds as many knobs as Freally Takelight can move at once; a module past that is refused with a message that says the rack is full.

v0.4.02026-10-23Tune

Pitch correction arrives: Tune pulls your vocal to the notes of its key with its formants kept, finds the key by itself, and drops to a few milliseconds of latency while you record; and the Shifter moves a voice up or down to two octaves.

Added

  • Tune — pitch correction from classic signal processing: Retune speed from 0 ms (the hard, stepped sound) to 400 ms, Humanize to let long notes breathe, and Flex to keep scoops and vibrato. Your voice keeps its character, because its formants stay where they are.
  • Auto Key — Tune listens to your vocal and settles on its key within about four seconds of singing, staying chromatic until then. Lock the key it hears with one click, or choose any of the 24 keys, or Chromatic.
  • The pitch graph — the last four seconds of your voice, the pitch sung and the pitch tuned, under the note it is pulled to.
  • Tracking — while your DAW records (or when you switch it on), Tune works at a few milliseconds of latency so you can sing through the chain; when you mix, it reports its full latency so your DAW keeps the vocal in time with the beat.
  • The Shifter — moves a voice up or down by up to two octaves, its formants moved on their own by up to an octave, mixed with the voice as it was: the ad-lib an octave down.
  • The take light listens — it glows amber while Auto Key is still listening for the key.

v0.3.02026-10-23Space

The four effect returns arrive, with delays, reverbs, doubling and width in time with your project, a Throw key on each return, and four more modules for the inserts.

Added

  • Four effect returns — A to D, each fed from the end of the inserts by its Send knob, with three slots and a fader of its own, joining the dry vocal on its way into the vocal bus.
  • Throws — hold a return's Throw key and the vocal is thrown into it, its send at 0 dB until you let go. Throw A to D are host parameters too, so your DAW can record a throw and play it back.
  • Delay — Digital, Tape, Analog, Ping-pong and Slap, set in milliseconds or as a note value in time with your project, with filters in the feedback, ducking under the vocal, and width.
  • Reverb — Plate, Room, Hall, Chamber and Ambience, with a decay from 0.1 to 20 seconds; and the Reverse Gate, a swelling reverse reverb that adds no latency.
  • Doubler, Chorus and Width — two to four voices from one take, a two-voice chorus, and a stereo width from mono to 200 % with a Haas delay.
  • Four more modules for the inserts — the Vari-mu Compressor with six time constants, the Level Rider, the Exciter and Lo-fi.
  • In time with your project — a delay set to a note value follows your DAW's tempo, even when it changes during the song.

v0.2.02026-10-14The Joint, the first modules & the docs site

The rack arrives: one Joint for a whole vocal chain, thirteen modules of Takelight's own design on rendered hardware, and the take light.

Added

  • The Joint — one rack in signal order: a front end of two slots, twelve inserts and a four-slot vocal bus, its latency reported to your DAW, with Amount blending the whole chain against the dry vocal.
  • Click-free changes — add, remove and swap modules while the track plays: the new rack is made ready away from the audio and crossfaded in over 30 milliseconds, a new character crossfades, and every knob glides.
  • Thirteen modules — Trim; the Parametric, Console and Program EQs; the Opto, FET, VCA and Punch compressors; the Vox Leveler; the De-esser; the Gate; Saturation; and a true-peak Limiter.
  • The rack screen — every module a card of rendered hardware, with its character, Bypass, Solo and gain reduction, every control reachable from the keyboard, in 18 languages.
  • Automation — each slot's 24 host parameters drive its module's controls in order, so the parameter list never changes.
  • The take light and the meters — the booth lamp, rendered in Blender in dark and light, glows green while a module is in the rack and your voice is coming in, and flashes red on a clip; In and Out meters with clip lights.
  • Report this — when Takelight itself fails, or after a crash, a page in your own browser shows everything a report would send, and nothing is sent until you press Send.
  • The docs site, complete — home, features, the manual, the changelog and the ladder, in 18 languages.

Changed

  • The licence agreement is now version 2, so Takelight asks you to accept it once more. It and the disclaimer are shorter and plainer.
  • One setting fewer — the coloured dots beside values are gone, and so is their setting. The credits list only licence notices.

v0.1.12026-10-13Settings, redrawn

Changed

  • Settings, redrawn — opened from a gear in the top bar and closed with an ×; the theme is a dark/light toggle with a moon and a sun, each short choice a segmented control with a sliding thumb, and About's long texts a list of rows.

v0.1.02026-10-12Foundation, CI spine & the release path

An empty Freally Takelight that loads in a host, passes audio through, and has every gate, both CI legs and the release path in place before a single feature exists.

Added

  • The plugin, empty, in every format — CLAP from nih-plug, VST3 and AU projected by clap-wrapper from the same binary, and a standalone. Mono and stereo inputs, stereo out; audio passes through unchanged.
  • The parameter pool — input, output, amount, bypass, tracking, three macros, four throws and 30 slots of 24 generic parameters, so loading a chain will never change the host's parameter list.
  • The editor shell — the Freally UI kit in dark and light, the EULA and disclaimer on first run, Settings and About, 18 languages with right-to-left layout.
  • The gates — no AI, no network, no borrowed code, product names and banned words, no downloads before 1.0, the ladder, licences, the shared crates, the UI kit and hardware art, locales and logical CSS, bindings drift, and no allocation on the audio thread.
  • Self-hosted CI — npm run ci:local (Windows) and npm run ci:linux (Docker Desktop), one gate list; Playwright against the editor and against the real standalone.
  • The release path — npm run release:local builds exactly the tag, validates the bundles, writes the release folder with checksums, prunes target/ to it and installs VST3 and CLAP through one UAC prompt.
  • The docs-site skeleton and the chain data skeleton (tlc validate, tlc import-roster).
