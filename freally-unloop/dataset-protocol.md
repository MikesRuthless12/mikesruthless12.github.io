# Dataset Protocol — Freally Unloop performance models

**Version 1.0 — 2026-09-24.** How an arrangement style is written down so the planner can perform it. Read before touching `data/performance/`. The schema is `data/schema/performance.schema.json`; every string vocabulary is in `data/schema/authored-vocabulary.json`; the compiler is `tools/unloopc`.

---

## 1. What you are writing

A **performance model** describes *how a record is arranged* in the style of one artist, producer or genre: which song forms it uses, how long its sections run, which parts play in which sections, which moves happen where (drop-outs, 808 cuts, sweeps, stutters, tape stops, switches, transition FX), how energy rises and falls, how its MIDI velocities should sit, and where a producer tag goes. It never contains notes, audio, lyrics or a specific song's arrangement.

**Ids are Freally MIDI Master's ids.** `metro-boomin`, `2hollis`, `trap` — the same keys as MIDI Master's `data/`, imported into `data/imported/roster.json`. A performance file whose `id` is not in the imported roster fails validation.

### Where a model goes

| Folder | Holds | `type` |
|---|---|---|
| `data/performance/genres/` | the 73 genre archetypes + `_default` — the inheritance floor every name stands on | `genre` |
| `data/performance/producers/` | flagship producers | `producer` |
| `data/performance/artists/` | flagship artists | `artist` |

`data/imported/` is generated by `unloopc import-roster` from a pinned MIDI Master tag and is **never hand-edited**.

### Tiers — what the badge on each name means

| Tier | What exists | What the planner uses |
|---|---|---|
| **flagship** | an Unloop performance file with its own modes and/or moves, `sources` and `notes` | the file, then its `extends` chain |
| **standard** | no Unloop file, but at least one of its modes performs the name's **own** MIDI Master forms or section bars (1,157 names at `shared-v1` and `shared-v2`, whose roster data is the same) | MIDI Master's forms and section bars + its genre's modes and moves |
| **inherited** | neither | its genre's performance model |

⛔ **The badge is derived from what the planner used, never declared.** `perform::resolve` records where every field of every mode came from, and the tier is read off those sources — so a name cannot be badged *standard* while no mode actually performs its own shape (`perform/tests/resolve.rs` holds the two together).

A name never fails to arrange. The tier is how the product stays honest about how much of the arrangement is *that* name's.

## 2. The legal and research rules — read before writing a single number

1. **Arrangement conventions are ideas, and ideas are free.** Section lengths, song forms, where a genre drops its drums, how long a tape stop lasts, when a beat switch tends to land — these are unprotectable conventions, the same category as tempo ranges and chord-progression families in MIDI Master's rules.
2. **Never encode a specific song's arrangement as a template.** A model describes a *tendency across a body of work*, expressed as probabilities and ranges. "This artist's hooks usually arrive by bar 9" is a convention; "the arrangement of *Song X*, bar by bar" is not allowed in any file, preset, template or example.
3. **Facts, not text.** Research reads tutorials, interviews, musicology and liner notes; models record the *facts* those sources establish, in numbers. Never paste prose from a source into `notes`.
4. **Every number has a source or a stated method.** Flagships list `sources` (pointers into `docs/research/arrangement-research.md`, which in turn cites the public source). Numbers from listening analysis name the method ("counted by ear across N releases, 2019–2024") in the research chapter.
5. **Names are descriptive.** "In the style of" only. No likeness, logo, signature or cover art anywhere, and nothing in a model reads as endorsement.
6. **Never attribute a source you did not use.** A false provenance line is worse than a missing one (MIDI Master removed a credit for exactly this reason). `docs/credits.md` lists every source that informed a number — and nothing else.
7. **Research stays private.** `docs/research/` is gitignored; the models it produces are public JSON.

## 3. The encoding checklist

Work these in order for every model.

### 3.1 Identify the lane
Find the name in `data/imported/roster.json`: its kind, era, genres and MIDI Master `extends`. Read its MIDI Master `arrangement` block if it has one — that is the starting point, not something to contradict without a source.

### 3.2 Pick `extends`
Genres extend nothing or a parent genre (`dark-trap` → `trap`). Artists and producers extend the genre(s) their arrangement practice actually follows, in priority order. Use MIDI Master's `extends` unless the arrangement research says otherwise — and then say so in `notes`.

### 3.3 Author the modes — a genre is never one arrangement
Every **genre** model authors **at least two modes**, target three or more, unless there is genuinely only one way the genre is arranged — in which case it sets `"singleMode": { "reason": "…" }` and has exactly one mode. The gates:
- a genre with fewer than two modes and no reason **fails**;
- a `singleMode.reason` on a model with two or more modes **fails** (the excuse outlived its key);
- two modes of one model whose forms **and** move kinds are both ≥0.8 similar (Jaccard) **fail** `unloopc distinct` — that is one mode written twice.

**What makes a mode a different way.** At least two of these must differ between any two modes of a model: the form family (hook-first vs intro-verse vs extended/DJ vs two-part switch), the section-length regime, the intro/outro regime, the move vocabulary, the energy shape.

**Standard mode ids** (reuse them where they fit so the UI can localise the names; new ids are allowed and go into the vocabulary):

| id | Name | Typical shape |
|---|---|---|
| `radio` | Radio cut | short intro (≤ `introMaxSeconds`), early hook, ≤ ~3:00, tight moves |
| `mixtape` | Mixtape cut | intro into a verse, longer verses, fewer FX |
| `club` | Club / DJ cut | phrase-grid intro and outro (16/32 bars), long builds, drop-centred |
| `switchup` | Switch-up | two-part record with a beat switch at the style's usual point |
| `minimal` | Minimal | few moves, space for vocals, drop-outs only where they matter |
| `extended` | Extended | an extra hook or a breakdown, longer outro |
| `stripped` | Stripped | melody-led, drums late or light, soft transitions |

Genre-specific ids in the vocabulary as of `v0.2.0`: `classic`, `posse`, `beattape` (boom bap), `slowjam`, `interlude` (2000s R&B), `vocal` (liquid DnB). A new id is added in `perform/src/vocab.rs` (`MODE_IDS`, with its English name) and reaches `authored-vocabulary.json` through `npm run dataset:schema`; a misspelt id fails to load.

Artists and producers inherit their genre's modes and may **override** a mode by id or **add** their own (an era, a signature form). A genre's modes are its complete list: a genre that `extends` another does not also collect the parent's modes (it inherits their *fields* — § 3.12).

### 3.4 Forms and section bars
`forms` are weighted section sequences using the section-kind vocabulary (`intro`, `verse`, `prehook`, `hook`, `bridge`, `breakdown`, `build`, `drop`, `switch`, `outro`). `sectionBars` gives, per kind, a fixed count (`16`), an inclusive range (`[4, 8]`) or weighted values (`{ "values": [16, 24, 32], "weights": [4, 1, 1] }`) — the three shapes MIDI Master's blocks use.

- **Ranges fit the phrase grid.** `[4, 8]` means 4 or 8, not 5–7: a range yields its multiples of 4, or of 2 when it holds none, or every whole number when it holds neither. A fixed count or a weighted list is used exactly as written.
- **A range may start at 0**, meaning the section is sometimes left out (`intro: [0, 2]` — no intro, or a short one). A fixed `0` is refused: a section that is never there belongs out of the form. (MIDI Master's engine floors such a draw at 1 bar; Unloop reads the author's evident meaning.)
- If a mode authors no forms it inherits them (§ 3.12). MIDI Master's `chorus`/`prechorus` arrive as `hook`/`prehook`, exactly as its own engine reads them; a block giving the two spellings different bars stops the import for a decision.
- `introMaxSeconds` caps the intro in whole bars at the plan's tempo; MIDI Master's `dropByBar` caps it too, but only when the mode did not state its own intro bars. A cap under one bar removes the intro, with a note on the plan.
- `hookByBar` shortens what comes before the first hook — inside each section's own options, longest first — and leaves a `hookLate` note when the form cannot allow it.
- `songLengthBars { min, max, mode }` (model-level): the record's length when nobody asks (`mode`) and the range a requested length is held inside. The planner lands within one section of a target by repeating the form's verse-to-hook cycle before the outro or removing repeats (never the intro, the closing outro or the first of any kind), and notes when the form cannot reach it.

### 3.5 Tempo
A mode may set `bpm: { min, max, mode }` when its tempo differs from the model's imported `session.bpm` (e.g. a club mode faster than the radio mode). This is what the tempo chip suggests and what *Adhere* stretches to. The planner reads `mode` as the tempo time-based rules assume when the host gives none; `min` and `max` are the tempo chip's (Phase 2).

### 3.6 Role activity
For each section kind, the probability each role plays and in which variant. Role names: `drums`, `kick`, `snare`, `hats`, `perc`, `bass`, `melody`, `counter`, `chords`, `vocal`, `fx`, and `all` (the whole loop). Variants: `hatsOnly`, `noOpenHats`, `noKick` (on `drums` — `noOpenHats` also on `hats`), `filtered` (with, and only with, a `filter` such as `lp:900`), `halfTime`, `fadeIn`, `fadeOut` (anywhere).

- **Fallback:** a part a section does not mention takes the nearest family that it does — `kick`/`snare`/`hats`/`perc` → `drums` → `all`, `counter`/`chords` → `melody` → `all`, the rest → `all`. So a genre can write `drums` and still drive a hats-only instance correctly. A part nothing mentions **plays** (subtractive arrangement: everything is in unless the model takes it out).
- **Parts sharing a family share one draw** — `drums: { prob: 0.2 }` brings the whole kit in or leaves it out, never a kick without its snare.
- `fadeIn`/`fadeOut` become the clip's fade over the whole section — the same fade the timeline's handles edit, not a second representation.
- The kit, the whole loop, the three One-Loop bands and the percussive/harmonic stems are *derived* from the ten parts, never authored.

### 3.7 Moves
Each move rule: `type`, `roles`, `at` (anchor), a length (`beats` or `bars`, fixed or `[min, max]`), `prob`, and type-specific parameters. Anchors:

| Anchor | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `bar:N` | bar N of the song |
| `section:K` | the whole of each section of kind K |
| `beforeSection:K` | ending exactly at the downbeat of each section of kind K |
| `lastBarBefore:K` | the bar before each section of kind K |
| `endOfSection:K` | ending at the end of each section of kind K |
| `startOfSection:K` | starting at the downbeat of each section of kind K |
| `fraction:a-b` | somewhere between fractions a and b of the song (e.g. `0.55-0.72`) |
| `everyBars:N` | ending at every N-bar boundary inside each section |

- **Lengths.** `beats` count in sixteenths (0.25 steps; a range of whole numbers steps by whole beats), `bars` in whole bars. `section:K` and `lastBarBefore:K` fix the length themselves, and a `switch` runs to the end of the record — authoring a length beside any of them is refused, since nothing would read it. With no length, each kind has a default (the vocabulary's `defaultBeats`). A move ending at a section boundary never takes all of the section before it: it keeps at least a bar, or half.
- **Parameters.** Each kind reads exactly the parameters the vocabulary lists for it (`filterSweep`: `shape`, `from`, `to`, `curve`; `stutterBuild`/`snareRoll`/`hatRoll`: `grid`; `gate`/`pump`: `rate`, `depth`; `pitchDive`: `semitones`; `switch`: `mode`), and a parameter the kind does not read is refused. `roles` are required, except on a `switch` (the whole loop) and the synthesized transition FX (`riser`, `downlifter`, `impact`, `reverseSwell`, `subDrop` — their own lane), where they are refused.
- **Merging.** A more specific model's rule replaces the rule with the same `type` and `at` it inherits (one `switch` per record, whatever its anchor); `"prob": 0` removes an inherited rule.
- **Exclusivity.** Moves that replace what a role plays (cuts, drop-outs, stutters, rolls, tape stops, reverses, gates, throws, dives) cannot overlap on one part; the higher priority wins (tape stop > rewind > drop-out > half time > cut > reverse > stutter > rolls > …). Processing (sweeps, lo-fi, pump) stacks, but not with its own kind.
- **One-Loop.** Every move carries what a single instance splitting one loop into three bands does with it. By default: `kick`/`bass` → low, `snare`/`melody`/`chords`/`vocal` → mid, `hats`/`perc`/`counter`/`fx` → high, and `drums` → low + high (the kit's weight is at the band edges; its snare shares the mids with the melody). A rule may give its own substitute (`"oneLoop": "bandLow+bandHigh"`) or `"oneLoop": "skip"`; either way the substitution is recorded on the move, never silent.
- A move whose parts are silent for its whole span is not placed.

### 3.8 Ear candy
`earCandy: { everyBars, pool, prob }` — small moves (`hatMute`, `reverseCrash`, `filterDip`, `reverseBeat`, `delayThrow`) that keep long sections alive: at every `everyBars` boundary inside a section, on the last beat before it, drawn from the pool, skipped where the part it acts on is silent. (A per-slot `energy` target curve is not read until the energy view and the length fitter, `v0.9.0`; the loader refuses it until then.)

### 3.9 Velocity policy (for the MIDI template)
Per role: `measured`, `tiered` (bands for accent/main/ghost), `fixed` (value), `flat` (value + top-voice boost), `contoured` (band + arch), or `artistFeel`. Genre defaults: drums `tiered` with MIDI Master's humanize tiers (accents 100–127, mains 76–89, ghosts 38–51), bass/808 `fixed` 127 (boom bap and live-band genres often `measured` or a narrower `tiered`), chords `flat` 96 (+6 top voice), melody `contoured`, counter `contoured` −12.

### 3.10 Tag anchors
`tag: { anchors, treatment, prob }` — where the producer's own tag lands and how (`dry`, `reverbThrow`, `delayThrow`, `filtered`, `reversed`). A tag lands at a point — `bar:N`, `beforeSection:K` (the bar before) or `startOfSection:K` — and only at each anchor's **first** occurrence: a tag is a signature, not a loop.

### 3.11 Provenance
`confidence` (`high`/`medium`/`low`), `sources` (research pointers), `notes` (why the numbers are what they are, in your own words). **Required for every file whose tier is flagship** — which is every file unless it lowers itself with `"tier": "standard"` — and shown on the model card.

### 3.12 How a mode resolves — where each field comes from
A name resolves through its **chain**: itself, then its parents in priority order (its file's `extends`, else MIDI Master's), each once, and `_default` last. Each field of a mode — forms, each section kind's bars, each part in each section, tempo, `introMaxSeconds`, `hookByBar`, ear candy — is taken from the first of:

1. **statements** — this mode, wherever the chain authors it as something other than a model's default mode (the floor's `radio` included);
2. **tendencies**, most specific model first — each model's *default mode* (its typical record), then its MIDI Master block;
3. **the floor's default mode**, below everything.

So a statement about a mode is never replaced by a name's tendency (a `radio` stays hook-first), while a name's own MIDI Master forms *do* outrank its genre's typical record. **Moves** are a list, taken whole from the statements if any author moves, else from the typical records (merged by key); a mode no Unloop model above the floor gives moves to performs the floor's, with MIDI Master's transitions laid over them — `dropOutBeats` as a whole-loop drop-out before each hook, `switchUpProb` as a switch-up near the middle (half-time or reordered, the two switches one loop allows), `fillBeforeSection: false` as no fills. `unloopc card <id>` prints where each mode's forms came from.

### 3.13 The gates a model meets
`npm run dataset:validate` (`unloopc validate`) refuses, each naming the file, the key path and the value: an unknown key; a string outside its vocabulary; a key the loader knows but **the planner does not read** (`perform/src/keys.rs`: every read key is in `READ_KEYS`, and `perform/tests/read_keys.rs` proves each one changes a plan when it changes; a key other code reads — `tier`, `singleMode`, a mode's `name`, read by the loader, resolution and the card — is in `READ_OUTSIDE_THE_PLAN` with its reader, and a test proves each reader reads it; anything else needs a `NOT_PLAN_LEVEL` excuse with its reason); a flagship without sources and notes; a genre with fewer than two modes and no reason, or a reason on two; two modes one way written twice; an `id` not in the roster; an `extends` that is not a genre; and anything that stops a name from resolving.

## 4. A worked example — the trap genre

Illustrative of the shape; the shipped file is authored from `docs/research/arrangement-research.md` § Trap.

```jsonc
{
  "$schema": "../../schema/performance.schema.json",
  "id": "trap", "type": "genre", "confidence": "medium",
  "sources": ["arrangement-research §2.1 Trap"],
  "notes": "Subtractive arrangement over a 4-bar loop: sparse intro, full hooks, stripped verses, cuts before downbeats. Section bars and the drop-out/switch probabilities start from MIDI Master's trap arrangement block.",
  "modes": [
    { "id": "mixtape", "weight": 3,
      "forms": [ { "sections": ["intro","verse","hook","verse","hook","bridge","hook","outro"], "weight": 3 },
                 { "sections": ["intro","hook","verse","hook","verse","hook","outro"], "weight": 2 } ],
      "sectionBars": { "intro": [4, 8], "verse": 16, "hook": [8, 16], "bridge": [4, 8], "outro": [4, 8] },
      "hookByBar": 25,
      "roles": {
        "intro":  { "melody": { "prob": 1.0, "variant": "filtered", "filter": "lp:900" }, "drums": { "prob": 0.2, "variant": "hatsOnly" }, "bass": { "prob": 0.0 } },
        "verse":  { "drums": { "prob": 1.0, "variant": "noOpenHats" }, "bass": { "prob": 1.0 }, "melody": { "prob": 0.7 }, "counter": { "prob": 0.2 } },
        "hook":   { "drums": { "prob": 1.0 }, "bass": { "prob": 1.0 }, "melody": { "prob": 1.0 }, "counter": { "prob": 0.8 } },
        "bridge": { "drums": { "prob": 0.1 }, "bass": { "prob": 0.2 }, "melody": { "prob": 1.0 } },
        "outro":  { "melody": { "prob": 1.0, "variant": "fadeOut" }, "drums": { "prob": 0.3 } }
      },
      "moves": [
        { "type": "cut",         "roles": ["hats"],  "at": "beforeSection:hook",  "beats": 2,      "prob": 0.7 },
        { "type": "dropOut",     "roles": ["drums","bass"], "at": "beforeSection:hook", "beats": [1, 2], "prob": 0.6, "oneLoop": "bandLow+bandHigh" },
        { "type": "filterSweep", "roles": ["melody"],"at": "section:intro", "shape": "lp", "from": 900, "to": 18000, "curve": 0.4, "prob": 0.8 },
        { "type": "stutterBuild","roles": ["drums"], "at": "lastBarBefore:hook", "grid": ["1/8","1/16","1/32"], "prob": 0.25 },
        { "type": "tapeStop",    "roles": ["all"],   "at": "endOfSection:bridge", "beats": [1, 2], "prob": 0.2 }
      ],
      "earCandy": { "everyBars": 8, "pool": ["hatMute","reverseCrash","filterDip"], "prob": 0.5 } },
    { "id": "radio", "weight": 2, "introMaxSeconds": 8, "hookByBar": 9,
      "forms": [ { "sections": ["intro","hook","verse","hook","verse","hook","outro"], "weight": 1 } ],
      "sectionBars": { "intro": 4, "verse": 16, "hook": 16, "outro": 4 } },
    { "id": "switchup", "weight": 1,
      "forms": [ { "sections": ["intro","hook","verse","hook","switch","verse","hook","outro"], "weight": 1 } ],
      "moves": [ { "type": "switch", "mode": ["halfTime","reorder","altLoop"], "at": "fraction:0.55-0.72", "prob": 1.0 } ] },
    { "id": "minimal", "weight": 1,
      "moves": [ { "type": "dropOut", "roles": ["drums"], "at": "beforeSection:hook", "beats": 1, "prob": 0.5 } ],
      "earCandy": { "everyBars": 16, "pool": ["hatMute"], "prob": 0.3 } }
  ],
  "velocityPolicy": { "drums": "tiered", "bass": { "fixed": 127 }, "chords": { "flat": 96, "topVoice": 6 }, "melody": "contoured", "counter": { "contoured": -12 } },
  "tag": { "anchors": ["bar:1", "beforeSection:hook"], "treatment": ["dry", "reverbThrow"], "prob": 0.7 }
}
```

A flagship artist or producer file is usually much shorter: it `extends` its genre, overrides one or two modes (a signature form, a characteristic intro), adds its signature moves with their probabilities, and cites its research.

## 5. Quality bars per tier

| Bar | Flagship | Genre |
|---|---|---|
| Modes | inherits the genre's; overrides or adds where research shows a difference | ≥2, target ≥3, distinct |
| Sources | required, pointing to a research section | recommended |
| Notes | required — why the numbers are what they are | recommended |
| Signature moves | ≥3 moves with probabilities that differ from the genre's | the genre's vocabulary |
| Listen test | 3 fixture loops, 5 seeds each, recognisably different from the genre archetype | 3 fixture loops, every mode |
| Card review | `unloopc card <id>` reads correctly in plain words | same |

---

## 6. Batch workflow

1. **Research** the batch in `docs/research/arrangement-research.md` (one chapter section per name or genre), with sources.
2. **Author** the JSON per § 3.
3. **Compile:** `npm run dataset:validate`, then `npm run dataset:distinct` and `npm run dataset:coverage`. Read what a mode performs with `cargo run -q -p unloopc -- plan <id> --mode <mode> --seed <hex>`.
4. **Read the card:** `npm run dataset:card -- <id>` — would a producer recognise the description?
5. **Listen:** generate on the three fixture loops (a trap loop, an R&B loop, a DnB loop) across five seeds per mode; the result must sound like the style and unlike the genre default where the model claims a difference.
6. **Commit** the batch with its research, one batch per commit; the phase closeout covers the rest.

---

## 7. The two checks: the compiler, then the ear

The compiler (`unloopc` plus the planner's gates — `READ_KEYS`, vocabulary parse, modes, distinctness, coverage) proves the file is *read*. Only the ear proves it is *right*. A model that passes every gate and sounds like the genre default has not earned its flagship tier — mark it `standard` in `notes` until it does.

---

## 8. Coordinating with Freally MIDI Master's dataset

- Unloop imports roster identity, aliases, eras, genres, `extends`, `arrangement` blocks and `session.bpm`/`halfTime` from a **pinned MIDI Master tag** (`data/imported/VERSION`). Re-import only when MIDI Master tags a dataset release; review the diff (new names, changed forms) in the same commit.
- A contradiction between Unloop's research and MIDI Master's `arrangement` block is resolved in **one** place: if MIDI Master's numbers are wrong, fix them in MIDI Master (with its gates) and re-import; if Unloop's performance layer legitimately differs (a move, not a form), say why in `notes`.
- Names added to MIDI Master arrive in Unloop at the next import as *standard* or *inherited* — they arrange immediately.

---

## 9. The coverage ledger

`unloopc coverage data` prints, per genre: modes (count and ids), flagships in that genre, standard and inherited counts; and overall: 73/73 genres, flagships ≥300, 2,413/2,413 resolving, distinctness failures, single-mode genres with reasons. The `v0.10.0` rung's gate is this ledger at target; the monthly post-1.0 dataset drops report against it.
