This is Flipcopy’s manual: every part of the window, every setting and every key, with an annotated screenshot beside each procedure. It grows with every version.

Getting started

Flipcopy runs inside your DAW as a CLAP, VST3 or AU plugin, or on its own as a standalone app. Put it on any audio track: it passes the track's audio through exactly as it arrives, so it is safe to leave in place.

The standalone app runs one at a time: open it again and its window comes to the front. It reopens where you left it. In Settings, Audio output chooses the device it plays through, from the next time it starts. Open a file with Flipcopy (Open with in Explorer, or a double-click once Flipcopy is the program for that kind of file) and it goes on the glass, ready for START; if Flipcopy is already open, that window takes it.

On Linux, a plugin's window is drawn by WebKitGTK, the weakest part of any Linux plugin. If Flipcopy's window misbehaves in your DAW, use the standalone app beside it: it does everything the plugin does except Scan from track, and its pages drag onto your tracks the same way.

There are no downloads before version 1.0.0, and the source code is private.

The licence agreement

The first time Flipcopy opens it shows its licence agreement. Read it to the end and Agree becomes available. Until you agree, Flipcopy reads nothing, copies nothing and adds nothing to your track's audio.

Your answer is kept on your computer and shared by every open copy of Flipcopy, in every DAW and in the standalone app. If you decline, choose Read it again whenever you are ready.

The window

On one side is the browser, with the preview player docked under it. The rest of the window is the stage, where the copier stands. Drag the line between them to make the browser wider or narrower, or focus it and use the arrow keys.

Press Ctrl+B (Cmd+B on a Mac), or the button at the browser’s edge, to fold the browser and its preview away: the stage takes the whole window. Press it again and they come back at the width you left them. Flipcopy remembers which you chose.

Making a copy

Put an audio or MIDI file on the glass: drop it from Explorer or Finder anywhere on the window, drag it from the browser or the preview strip onto the copier, press Enter on a row in the browser, or press Enter on the empty glass to choose one. The copier prints the original and lays it face-down on the glass. Putting a file on the glass never starts a copy.

Press the green START key, or Ctrl+Enter (Cmd+Enter on a Mac). The lid closes, the lamp sweeps, and the copy slides out onto the tray: an audio loop comes out as MIDI, one page for each part the copier hears; a MIDI file comes out as audio, one page for each part. Esc or the red STOP key cancels it; the grey HOME key clears the glass.

Every page is a real file in your Copies folder (Documents/Freally Flipcopy/Copies, unless you choose another in Settings), numbered, and never moved, overwritten or deleted. Grab a page by the lime frame round its printed clip and drag it straight onto a track in your DAW, or drag the whole stack at once.

The copier’s sounds play through the plugin’s own output, quietly, never while your DAW plays and never into a bounce. Turn them off or down in Settings.

The machine behaves like one: it gives a small knock when the lid lands, a soft shadow falls where each copy bends out of the slit, and every copy lies at its own slight angle. Settings → Motion → Reduce takes the knock away.

Audio → MIDI

Put an audio loop on the glass and press START: the copier listens to it and prints a MIDI page for each part it hears, in this order: drums, bassline, melody, countermelody and chords. A part it finds nothing in gets no page, and a loop with no notes in it at all is refused, never copied as a blank page.

The drums are written on the lanes of a General MIDI kit, kick, snare, hats and the rest, so they play on any drum rack, and their page prints a row for each voice. The bassline, the melody and the countermelody are each one line. The chords are written as a progression, which chord comes when, not the voicing that was played, and their page says so: PROGRESSION - NOT THE PLAYED VOICING. A page prints the loop’s key only when it was read clearly.

A sung line is not one of those parts. With Parts set to Every part, a page each, the copier hears a vocal, leaves it alone and says so on the first page: A VOCAL WAS HEARD AND LEFT ALONE. To copy the vocal itself, choose Parts → Vocal line, one note at a time: the sung line comes out as one MIDI part, one note at a time. A loop that is nothing but a sung line, copied with every part, is refused, and the screen says why.

The copier takes loops of up to 16 bars: one, two, four and eight-bar loops are all copied. A longer clip is refused on the glass with its length, never cut short to fit. So is a file that is silent, damaged or not audio the copier can read.

A copy’s tempo is the one the loop was played at, read from the audio, and its pages print it with READ beside it. Your project’s tempo only decides between two readings of the same loop, such as 70 and 140 BPM; it becomes the copy’s tempo only when nothing in the loop keeps a beat, and then the page says so. In the standalone app, the tempo in the file’s name, such as Loop 90 BPM.wav, does the same, or 120 when the name has none. Copy Settings → Tempo can choose instead: the project’s, the one in the file name, or one you set.

Every page is a file, and a file can be copied again. Drag an audio page from the tray back onto the glass, or drop one from your Copies, and press START: it is copied back into MIDI, and the new copy says which page it came from. Because every audio page ends on a bar line, a page read back keeps the tempo it was written at.

MIDI → audio

Put a MIDI file on the glass and press START: the copier plays each part of it through an instrument and prints an audio page for each part, the drums, the bassline, the melody, the countermelody and the chords, as the file has them. With the pages comes the Complete mix, every part played together: drag it by the COMPLETE MIX tab at the foot of the page on top, or every page at once by ALL PARTS. Copy Settings → Pages → Mix only makes the Complete mix alone.

Each pitched part plays through the instrument you choose for it in Copy Settings, Preview Piano unless you choose another, and the drums play through a drum machine: Preview Kit, a TR-909, unless you choose the 808, the 707, the 606 or a kit of your own one-shots. Every audio page prints its instrument on its TONER line. Pitch bend and the sustain pedal are played; any other controller in the file is listed on the page as not rendered, never dropped without a word.

Tail sets how long the sound rings on after the last note. Then the file runs on to the next bar line of the file’s own meter, so every audio page is a whole number of bars long: dropped into your DAW, it sits on the grid and loops cleanly, and it carries its tempo for any DAW that reads it.

Each page is written as WAV, AIFF, FLAC or MP3 (320 kbps), at the project’s sample rate or at 44.1, 48, 88.2 or 96 kHz, in 16 or 24-bit or 32-bit float. If a page would clip, the copier turns it down by a fixed amount, just enough to keep it under −0.3 dBFS, and prints that gain on the page. It never limits or normalises: a quiet file stays quiet.

Reading a page

A page whose notes are all drums prints one row for each voice of the kit — kick, snare, closed hat and the rest — named in your language and in the Studio’s order, instead of a row for every pitch. A drum the kit has no voice for keeps a row of its own, named by its General MIDI note.

The pages in the tray are printed small. Rest the pointer on one and, after a moment, a lens shows the page around the pointer, its facts large enough to read. It follows the pointer, never appears while you drag, and goes the moment you press.

The touch screen

The copier’s screen says what it is doing and which way it copies: AUDIO > MIDI or MIDI > AUDIO. Under it are its soft keys. SETTINGS opens Copy Settings; ◀ PREV and NEXT ▶ page through your copies; SAVE, REVEAL and REPRINT work on the copy in the tray. None of them starts a copy: only START does.

When a MIDI file has no tempo of its own but its name says one, such as Hook 127 BPM.mid, a chip beside the screen offers it. Use 127 BPM copies that file at that tempo; Keep the project’s tempo leaves it. Flipcopy never changes your DAW’s tempo, and every page prints its tempo and where that tempo came from.

Copy Settings

Press SETTINGS on the touch screen and the panel grows out of it. Every setting changes the copy you make, and a change is for the next copy, never one already being made; your project keeps them. Tab moves through the panel, the arrow keys choose, and Esc closes it.

For an audio loop, Parts chooses what to copy: every part, a page each; one part only, the drums, bassline, melody or chords; or the vocal line, one note at a time. If the loop has none of the part you asked for, the copier says so instead of printing a blank page. Grid puts the notes on 1/8, 1/16, 1/32 or 1/16T, or leaves them as they were read, and Swing keeps a swing or straightens it.

For a MIDI file, Pages chooses a page per part or the mix only, and Tail sets how long the sound rings on after the last note. Format writes WAV, AIFF, FLAC or MP3 (320 kbps), at the project’s sample rate or at 44.1, 48, 88.2 or 96 kHz, in 16 or 24-bit or 32-bit float. FLAC and AIFF have no float, so they write 24-bit; MP3 is written at 44.1 or 48 kHz.

Both ways, Tempo is Automatic, the project’s, the file’s own, from the file name, or one you set.

Instruments

A MIDI copy plays each part through an instrument from the Freally library, the same library Freally MIDI Master uses. In Copy Settings, type in a part’s search box to find one by its name, genre or family, add a number to ask for one of its variations, and pick it: the bassline, melody, countermelody and chords can each have their own. Preview Piano is the default. The drums play through the drum machine you choose: Preview Kit by default, or the 808, the 707 or the 606.

Every audio page prints the instrument it was played with on its TONER line, beside its tempo.

Your copies

◀ PREV and NEXT ▶ on the touch screen, or the [ and ] keys, page through every copy you have made; each lies in the tray, ready to drag. SAVE saves the page on top as a new file, wherever you choose and in the format you pick. REVEAL shows the copy in its folder. REPRINT puts the copy’s original back on the glass with the settings it was made with: press START to make it again.

Drag a page back onto the glass, or drop one from your Copies, and it goes on as an original, ready to be copied the other way; the new copy says which page it came from. To drag several pages at once, Shift-click them, then drag one: they all go, in page order.

Every copy’s folder holds a ticket.txt, a plain-text record of how it was made: the original and its fingerprint, the settings, the tempo and where it came from, the instruments, and every page’s file and fingerprint.

The Studio

Above the machine are two tabs, COPIER and STUDIO. Press Ctrl+1 or Ctrl+2 (Cmd+1 or Cmd+2 on a Mac) to reach them from anywhere in the window, or move between the tabs with the arrow keys. Switching never stops anything: a copy being scanned carries on behind the Studio, and its pages are out when you come back. Each instance of the plugin keeps its own tab.

To open a copy, press STUDIO on the touch screen to open the copy in the tray, or select a file under Copies in the browser and press Open in Studio. A copy of an audio loop opens its MIDI pages, one part tab each, in page order; a copy of a MIDI file opens the parts of the MIDI it was made from. The notes are read from the files the copy wrote, so a copy whose files have moved or changed does not open, and the Studio says why. If the copy already open has changes you haven’t sent, opening another asks first.

Each part is edited in Freally MIDI Master’s piano roll. Double-click to draw a note, and drag a note to move it. Drag its left or right edge, where the pointer becomes [ or ], to shorten or lengthen it. Select notes with a box, with Shift-click or with Ctrl-click; copy, cut and paste them; clone them with Shift+D or by dragging with Alt; delete them with Delete, Backspace or a right-click; and nudge them with the arrow keys.

The velocity lane sits underneath, and Snap chooses the grid that notes snap to. Ctrl+Z undoes, and Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y redoes (Cmd on a Mac). A drum part shows its kit voices by name down the side. To change part, click its tab, or use the arrow keys, Home and End once a part tab has focus.

Play, Stop and Loop play the parts, and every part has S to solo it and M to mute it. What plays is exactly the file the copier will be given, through the instruments the copy prints with, and a part you solo sounds exactly as its page of the copy will. Change a part’s instrument right there, and the drums’ drum machine too.

Send to the copier writes the edit as a new MIDI file in the Studio folder of your Copies folder, never over another file, puts it on the glass and shows the copier. Nothing is copied until you press the green START; then the edit is copied like any original, and its copy’s ticket says which copy it was edited from. A part you emptied gets no page, and an edit with no notes at all is not sent: the Studio says why. When the edit holds one part only, its file is named for that part, such as Loop - Countermelody - Edit of copy 4 - 120 BPM.mid, so the copier copies it as that part.

The mixer and your own sounds

The Studio has three views, Notes, Mixer and Sound, in the tabs at its top right; the arrow keys move between them. Every fader, knob and choice in them belongs to the project's Copy Settings, so what the Studio plays is exactly what START prints, and it is saved with the project.

The Mixer has a strip for every part of the open copy and one for the master. A pitched part's strip has Freally MIDI Master's channel strip: Low, Mid and High, the Room and Echo sends, Heat and Pitch Mod. Under it are the pan knob, the fader with its meter, and the S and M keys. The meter is green below −12 dBFS, yellow up to −3 and red above, and its clip light comes on at 0 dBFS and stays on until you click it. A part with its fader all the way down is not printed at all.

Drag a fader or a knob up and down, hold Shift to move it finely, or turn the mouse wheel over it. With it focused, the arrow keys move it a step, Home and End take it to either end, and a double-click puts it back: a fader to 0 dB, a knob to its own default. Every one says its value as it moves.

Sound shapes the part shown. It plays through the library's instrument, with Freally MIDI Master's SYNTH page beneath it: the oscillators, the sub and noise, the filter, both envelopes, the LFOs, the macros and the effects. Every knob you move is kept with the copy's settings, and Reset puts them all back. A star marks an instrument as a favourite, and the Starred list plays one on the part.

Your own samples come from the browser: select an audio file and press Add to your samples. A sample must be a file on this computer, audio of up to 20 seconds; its pitch is read when it is added, and it is checked again every time it is played, so a sample that has moved or changed is never played as something else. New sampler sound plays one of your samples across the keyboard, with its root note, tuning, envelope, filter and volume. For the drums, New kit makes a kit of your one-shots: choose one for a lane, or drag a one-shot from the browser onto the lane.

Your favourites, samples, kits and sampler sounds are kept in one instrument library that every Freally product on this computer shares. Flipcopy ships no samples: yours are read where they are.

The browser

The browser opens on its Categories: Copies, every page and mix you have made, and Recent, the originals you last put on the glass. Under them are the folders you have added; choose + Add Folder to add one, up to 128. A folder on a drive that is not plugged in keeps its place and says it isn’t connected right now. To stop browsing a folder, right-click it, or select it and press Delete; nothing on disk is touched.

Open a folder to walk it as a tree. Type to filter it by name, pick a tag or Starred to narrow it, and choose Show all files to see the sidecar files Flipcopy hides, such as .asd and .reapeaks. Back and Forward take you through the places you have been.

Walk the rows with ↑ and ↓: each row you land on plays once in the preview. → opens a folder and ← closes it. Enter puts the selected file on the glass, and so does a double-click on it, or dragging its row onto the copier.

The first time you add a folder, Flipcopy counts its files, with a bar that shows how far it has got. It reads at most 12 folders deep and 50,000 files, and never follows a shortcut out of the folder.

The preview player

The preview player sits under the browser. An audio file shows its real waveform. A MIDI file shows its notes and plays through Preview Piano, the same sound a copy uses unless you choose another, so you hear what the copy will sound like.

Space plays and pauses, Stop goes back to the start, and a click or a drag on the strip moves the playhead. Loop repeats the file, Play when selected (the headphones) plays each file you select, and the slider sets the level. The preview plays through Flipcopy’s own output and never into a bounce. A WAV file of any length plays; a file in another format plays if it is five minutes or shorter.

Click the original on the glass, or a page on the tray, to hear it. When a copy is finished, Copy plays all its pages together and Original plays the file on the glass, from the same place in the music, so you can hear the difference before you drag. Playing it is never required: the pages can be dragged as soon as the last one lands.

The strip wears the same lime frame as a page. Drag it onto the glass to put that file there.

In your DAW

Flipcopy is made to stay in a project: what is on its glass is saved with the project, it can scan a few bars straight from the track it sits on, its browser drags any file straight onto a track, and many copiers can run in one project at once.

Saved with your project

The original on the glass is kept with your DAW’s project, with the Copy Settings you chose. Reopen the project and the glass shows it again. If the file has moved since, the glass says The original has moved, and Locate… finds it.

A project whose Flipcopy state can’t be read opens with an empty glass and says so; nothing from another project is ever carried over. Settings saved by a newer version of Flipcopy are kept as they are, so opening a project in an older version and saving it again loses nothing.

Scan from track

In a DAW, Copy Settings has Scan from track. Choose 1 to 16 bars, press Arm, then press play in your DAW. As the track plays, its audio, or the MIDI notes it receives, is taken for that many bars, written as a new file in the Captures folder of your Copies folder, and put on the glass. Nothing is copied until you press the green START.

The bars are counted from your DAW’s tempo and meter: if it isn’t sending them yet, press play once, then arm. The panel says what a host won’t send. Ableton Live gives an audio effect no MIDI, so there a scan takes the track’s audio, never its notes; Logic may not run a track that isn’t live, so arm the track, or play it, before you scan. Nothing is scanned while your DAW bounces, and the standalone app has no track to scan: drop a file on the glass instead.

Drag a file straight into your DAW

At the end of every audio and MIDI file’s row in the browser, and on the Preview strip, is a grip: Drag the file into your DAW. Drag it onto a track and the file itself lands there, just as it is on your disk. The row itself still drags onto the glass, to be copied.

Whatever you drag out to your DAW, a page or a file, the picture under the pointer is that clip: its notes or its waveform, in the lime frame. A DAW that draws no drag picture shows its own file icon instead; the file is the same.

Many copiers at once

Put Flipcopy on as many tracks as you like: each copier keeps its own glass, its own Copy Settings and its own tab. Eight at once, each with its sounds, its preview and a scan running, use well under one percent of a 256-sample buffer, and a window with nothing moving runs no animation at all.

The standalone and Pro Tools

The standalone app is all of Flipcopy in a window of its own, playing through the audio output you choose in Settings: the copier, the browser and the preview, Copy Settings and the Studio. The one thing it lacks is Scan from track, which needs a DAW’s track; put a file on the glass instead. It is the way to use Flipcopy without a DAW, beside one, or with a DAW the plugin cannot load.

Pro Tools loads only AAX plugins, and Flipcopy has no AAX version, so it does not appear in Pro Tools. Run the standalone app beside Pro Tools instead: drag a page from the tray, or a file from the browser by its grip, straight onto a Pro Tools track. If a drag does not land, press SAVE on the touch screen, save the page wherever you like, and import it into Pro Tools.

Settings

Open Settings with the gear at the top of the browser. Its categories run down one side (General, Appearance, Sounds, Motion, Audio output, Copies folder, About), and the one you choose opens beside them.

Machine sounds turns the copier’s sounds on or off, sets their level and keeps them quiet while your DAW plays. A switch of its own turns off the soft sounds the panels and dialogs make as they open and close.

Under Motion, Quick runs the machine at its short timings, with every sound still in its place. Reduced motion follows your system’s setting, or choose Reduce to have it always: no flip, no swing of the lid, no sweep and no dimming of the room. The copier still shows each step and plays each sound, and START is still the only way to make a copy.

Copies folder shows where your copies go. Change… picks another folder: new copies go there, and the copies already made stay where they are. Show in folder opens it. About shows the version, the third-party licences and the licence agreement.

Light and dark

Flipcopy has a dark mode and a light mode, both in Freally green. In Settings, under Appearance, choose Dark or Light: each is shown by its own copier. The change is immediate, with no flash of the other colour, and it is kept on this computer, the same in the plugin and the standalone app.

The copier is rendered on each mode’s own ground, so it always stands in one room, with no box around it. The panels, the glass, the dialogs and the keys come from one design that every Freally app shares, and the knobs, faders, sliders, switches and keys are rendered for each mode. The standalone app’s title bar follows the mode too, with no title in it: the turning wordmark is the title.

Under Motion, Reduce lays the turning title flat: with reduced motion, whether you chose it or your system asks for it, the title stops square to you, level and still. Quick turns it in half the time, as it speeds up everything else, and reduced motion still wins over both. Under Sounds, Animation sounds turns the sounds of dialogs and panels opening and closing on or off, beside the copier’s own sounds.

Troubleshooting

Most problems say what they are on the copier’s touch screen. These are the common ones, and what to do about each.

A file is refused

The screen gives the reason. The copier takes loops of up to 16 bars, and refuses a file that is longer or larger than it takes, silent, damaged, or not audio or MIDI it can read. It also refuses a MIDI file with no notes, with more notes than it takes, that counts time in SMPTE frames, or that holds separate songs (format 2), and a file on a network share, which must be copied to this computer first. Shorten a long loop to 16 bars in your DAW and put that on the glass.

A sung line was left alone

With Parts set to Every part, a page each, a vocal is heard and left alone, and the first page says so. To copy the sung line, choose Parts → Vocal line, one note at a time, in Copy Settings, and press START again.

Nothing was found

When the screen says there is nothing in a clip to copy, the copier found no notes in it. If you asked for one part only, such as Drums only, and the loop has none of it, the screen says so instead of printing a blank page: choose Every part, a page each, or another part.

START does nothing

START copies what is on the glass, so put a file there first. While a copy is being made the copier is busy: wait for it, or press STOP. If the file on the glass changed or moved after you put it there, put it on the glass again. Ctrl+Enter presses START only while the COPIER tab is shown.

Flipcopy is not in your DAW’s plugin list

Make sure the plugin is in a folder your DAW scans for CLAP or VST3 plugins, or AU plugins on a Mac, then rescan your DAW’s plugins. Logic checks an AU the next time it starts. Pro Tools never lists Flipcopy: see The standalone and Pro Tools.

A drag doesn’t land in your DAW

Some hosts don’t take a file dragged out of a plugin’s window; when Flipcopy can tell, its screen says Dragging isn’t available here — use Save As instead. Press SAVE on the touch screen to save the page on top as a new file, wherever you like and in the format you pick, then import it; REVEAL shows the copy’s own file in its folder. From the keyboard, Tab reaches Save the copy as… and Show the copy in its folder too.

When something goes wrong

If Flipcopy itself fails, such as a copy it could not write, the screen offers Report. It opens a page in your web browser that shows exactly what the report holds; nothing is sent unless you press Send there. A file the copier refuses, because it is too long, silent or not audio, is not a failure, and the screen says why instead.

Languages

Flipcopy speaks eighteen languages. It starts in your system's language; to choose another, open Settings with the gear at the top of the browser and pick one. The choice applies to every copy of Flipcopy on the computer.

Privacy and the network

Flipcopy has no account and no telemetry, and the plugin and standalone app contain no networking code at all. Your files and your copies never leave your computer.

It contains no AI of any kind: every copy is made by classic, deterministic signal processing, and a build gate fails if a machine-learning library is ever added.

The one exception is an optional update check, and it is off until you say yes. The standalone app asks once; a DAW is never asked and never checks when it starts. A separate small program, not the plugin or the app, fetches one public file over HTTPS, freallyproducts.com/freally-flipcopy/version.json. It sends no ID, no version and nothing about your computer — only what any web request carries, your IP address. If a newer version is out, Flipcopy shows its notes and a button that opens this site; it never downloads or installs anything. Settings → Updates says when it last checked, and turns it on or off.

Building from source

Flipcopy’s source code is private: it is not published, there is no public repository, and no one outside the project can build it today.

For the record, a build needs Rust stable, at the exact version the source pins, and Node.js. npm ci installs the editor’s tools, and npm run plugin:build builds the editor and the plugin and copies the CLAP and the VST3, and the AU on a Mac, into your plugin folders.
